# Research: Person Systems (Grammatical Person + Clusivity)

## Executive Summary

Person systems encode the relationship between discourse participants (speaker, addressee, other) and their grammatical representation. While basic 1st/2nd/3rd person distinctions are universal, significant cross-linguistic variation exists in:

1. **Clusivity (Inclusive/Exclusive Distinction)**: Found in ~31.5% of world's languages (WALS data), nearly universal in Austronesian and Australian languages, absent in all European and Semitic languages. Critical for Bible translation in 1,000+ languages.

2. **Honorific Person Systems**: Grammatical encoding of social status/politeness (T-V distinction in European languages, complex keigo systems in Japanese/Korean). Not clusivity, but interacts with person marking.

3. **Obviative Systems**: Found in Algonquian and some other North American languages, distinguishing "proximate" (more salient) from "obviative" (less salient) third person—sometimes called "fourth person."

4. **Logophoric Pronouns**: Special third person forms in reported speech contexts, primarily West African languages.

**Theological Challenges**: Biblical source languages (Hebrew, Greek) lack clusivity distinction, requiring **interpretive decisions** for translation into clusivity-marking languages. Key contexts: Genesis 1:26 ("Let us" - Trinity?), "Our Father" prayers (inclusive/exclusive?), Pauline epistles ("we" = Paul alone? Paul + co-authors? Paul + readers?), Acts 15:25 (apostles excluding congregation).

## 1. Scholarly Sources

### 1.1 Cysouw, Michael (2013). "Inclusive/Exclusive Distinction in Independent Pronouns"

**Citation**: Cysouw, Michael. 2013. Inclusive/Exclusive Distinction in Independent Pronouns. In: Dryer, Matthew S. & Haspelmath, Martin (eds.) The World Atlas of Language Structures Online. Leipzig: Max Planck Institute for Evolutionary Anthropology. Available: https://wals.info/feature/39A

**Key Findings**:
- **200 languages surveyed**: 63 languages (31.5%) have full inclusive/exclusive distinction, 120 (60%) have no distinction (like English)
- **Distribution**: Minimal in Europe and Africa; concentrated in Asia (Dravidian, Munda, northeastern languages), Southeast Asia, Pacific (Austronesian - nearly universal), Australia (non-Pama-Nyungan), Americas (scattered)
- **Typology**: 5 categories - (1) No 'we', (2) 'We' same as 'I', (3) No inclusive/exclusive (English-type), (4) Only inclusive differentiated, (5) Full distinction (both forms)
- **Language Examples**: Type 5 includes Mandarin Chinese, Chamorro, Paamese, Yagua, Gooniyandi
- **Comparative**: Distinction not found in any European language

**Citation Code**: {cysouw-2013-wals39a}

**Relevance**: Establishes typological baseline - approximately 1/3 of world's languages require clusivity marking, making TBTA annotations critical for these languages.

### 1.19 Wikipedia Contributors (2024). "Honorific speech in Japanese"

**Citation**: Wikipedia contributors. 2024. Honorific speech in Japanese. Wikipedia, The Free Encyclopedia. Available: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honorific_speech_in_Japanese (Accessed 2025-11-26)

**Key Findings**:

**Relevance**: Japanese has extreme honorific person complexity but NO clusivity - demonstrates person systems vary orthogonally (honorifics ≠ clusivity).

## 3. Translation Case Studies

### Case Study 1: Genesis 1:26 - "Let us make man in our image"

**Verse**: בְּצַלְמֵנוּ כִּדְמוּתֵנוּ נַֽעֲשֶׂה אָדָם "Let us make man in our image, in our likeness" (Hebrew)

**Challenge**: How many persons in "us/our"? English ambiguous (2, 3, or many).

**Source Language**: Hebrew נַֽעֲשֶׂה (*na'aseh*) = cohortative 1st person plural "let us make". No clusivity distinction, no trial number marking.

**Translation Decisions for Trial-marking languages** (Austronesian/Oceanic with trial number):

| Language | Number Choice | Rationale |
|----------|--------------|-----------|
| Kilivila (PNG) | **Trial** (exactly 3) | Trinitarian interpretation {tbta-source/TRANSLATION-EDGE-CASES.md} |
| Larike (Maluku) | **Trial** (exactly 3) | Trinitarian interpretation |
| Fijian | **Trial** likely | Oceanic with trial number |

**Alternative interpretations**:
- **Dual** (exactly 2): HERETICAL - implies Binitarianism/Arianism (only 2 persons of Godhead)
- **Plural** (many, vague): Orthodox but less precise - acceptable if no trial number
- **Divine Council**: Jewish interpretation - God + angels (rejected by Christian orthodoxy - angels don't create per Isaiah 44:24)

**Clusivity question**: Is "us" **inclusive** or **exclusive**?
- {tbta-source/DATA-STRUCTURE.md}: "Person: 'First Inclusive' # 'us' including the listener"
- Who is the listener? If intra-Trinitarian dialogue, "inclusive" includes the other persons of Trinity being addressed
- Alternative: Exclusive = God speaking to humanity (but humanity not created yet, problematic)

**Theological Stakes**: **HIGH** - Trinity doctrine

**TBTA Annotation**: Number = Trial, Person = First Inclusive (per {tbta-source/DATA-STRUCTURE.md})

**Scholarly Sources**:
- {tbta-source/TRANSLATION-EDGE-CASES.md}: "Maintains Trinity reference in translation. Without TBTA, translators might use dual (2 persons) or generic plural (many persons), losing the theological precision."

### Case Study 2: Acts 15:25 - "It seemed good to us..."

**Verse**: ἔδοξεν ἡμῖν γενομένοις ὁμοθυμαδόν "It seemed good to us, being assembled with one accord..." (Greek)

**Challenge**: Does "us" (ἡμῖν *hēmin*) include the listeners (congregation) or exclude them?

**Source Language**: Greek ἡμῖν (*hēmin*) = 1st person plural dative "to us" (ambiguous)

**Context**: Apostles and elders in Jerusalem writing letter to Gentile believers in Antioch, Syria, Cilicia (Acts 15:23). The letter reports the Jerusalem Council's decision.

**Translation Decisions for Clusivity-marking languages**:

| Language | Clusivity Choice | Rationale |
|----------|-----------------|-----------|
| Tagalog | **Exclusive** (*kami*) | {tbta-source/TRANSLATION-EDGE-CASES.md}: "Apostles speaking, congregation listening" |
| Malay/Indonesian | **Exclusive** (*kami*) | Apostles only, not including addressees |
| Fijian | **Exclusive** | Jerusalem council, not Antioch congregation |
| Tok Pisin | **Exclusive** | {sil-1999-clusivity-nt} |

**Why Exclusive?**:
- Speakers: Apostles, elders, whole church in Jerusalem (Acts 15:22)
- Listeners: Gentile believers in Antioch, Syria, Cilicia
- "Us" = decision-makers in Jerusalem, NOT including the recipients of the letter

**Impact of Wrong Choice**: {tbta-source/TRANSLATION-EDGE-CASES.md}: "Using the wrong form would incorrectly imply the congregation participated in the apostolic decision-making."

**Theological Stakes**: MEDIUM - Apostolic authority, ecclesiology (who makes decisions for church?)

**TBTA Annotation**: Person = First Exclusive (Code: B)

**Scholarly Sources**:
- {tbta-source/TRANSLATION-EDGE-CASES.md}: Acts 15:25 documented example
- {sil-1999-clusivity-nt}: SIL translation recommendations

### Case Study 3: Matthew 6:9 / Luke 11:2 - "Our Father" (Lord's Prayer)

**Verse**: Πάτερ ἡμῶν ὁ ἐν τοῖς οὐρανοῖς "Our Father in heaven..." (Greek Matthew 6:9)

**Challenge**: Is "our" (ἡμῶν *hēmōn*) **inclusive** or **exclusive**?

**Source Language**: Greek ἡμῶν (*hēmōn*) = 1st person plural genitive "our" (ambiguous)

**Context**: Jesus teaching disciples to pray

**Translation Decisions for Clusivity-marking languages**:

**Option 1: EXCLUSIVE** (excluding God)
- "Our Father" = disciples' Father, not including the Father himself in "our"
- **Most translators choose exclusive** {sil-1999-clusivity-nt}
- Rationale: God is the addressee, not part of the "we" praying

**Option 2: INCLUSIVE** (including... whom?)
- If inclusive, who is included? Cannot include God (the addressee)
- Could include Jesus? (But Jesus is teaching the prayer, not praying it with them in this context)
- Could include all believers? (Inclusive of all who will pray this prayer)

**Historical Mistake**: {sil-1999-clusivity-nt}: "Early missionaries, not knowing this, used the inclusive form in the Lord's Prayer, 'Forgive us our trespasses (yours and ours).' This, of course, had to be corrected."

**Theological Stakes**: MEDIUM-HIGH - Prayer theology, relationship to God

**TBTA Annotation**: Person = First Exclusive (Code: B) (God not included in "our")

**Scholarly Sources**:
- {sil-1999-clusivity-nt}: SIL documentation of missionary error and correction

### Case Study 4: 1 Corinthians 1:1 - "Paul... and Sosthenes our brother"

**Verse**: Παῦλος... καὶ Σωσθένης ὁ ἀδελφός "Paul... and Sosthenes our brother" (Greek)

**Challenge**: When Paul uses "we" in the letter, does it include Sosthenes (co-author), or just Paul, or Paul + Sosthenes + Corinthian readers?

**Context**: Epistolary "we" - ambiguous in Greek, crucial for clusivity-marking languages

**Translation Decisions**:

**Option 1: EXCLUSIVE** ("we" = Paul + Sosthenes, NOT including Corinthian readers)
- {sil-1999-clusivity-nt}: SIL International (1999) recommends **exclusive**
- Rationale: "We" refers to Paul and the Christians with him (co-authors), not the letter recipients

**Option 2: INCLUSIVE** ("we" = Paul + Sosthenes + Corinthian readers)
- Velma Pickett & Florence Cowan recommend **inclusive**
- Rationale: Shared Christian experience, Paul including readers in "we"

**Complication**: Paul's "we" varies by context within the same letter:
- 1 Cor 3:9: "We are God's fellow workers" - probably exclusive (Paul + Apollos, not all Corinthians)
- 1 Cor 15:51: "We shall not all sleep, but we shall all be changed" - probably inclusive (all believers)

**Theological Stakes**: LOW-MEDIUM - Contextual precision, authorship

**Implication**: Each instance of "we" in epistles requires **separate analysis** - no blanket rule

**TBTA Challenge**: Must analyze each verse individually

**Scholarly Sources**:
- {sil-1999-clusivity-nt}: Documents disagreement between experts (Pickett/Cowan vs. SIL)

## 4. Verse Analysis: Key Biblical Passages Requiring Clusivity Determination

### 4.1 Old Testament - Plural First Person in Divine Speech

| Passage | Hebrew | English | Clusivity Question |
|---------|--------|---------|-------------------|
| **Genesis 1:26** | נַֽעֲשֶׂה "let us make" | "Let us make man..." | Inclusive (intra-Trinity) or exclusive? |
| **Genesis 3:22** | מִמֶּנּוּ "from us" | "Man has become like one of us" | Same as 1:26 - Trinity reference |
| **Genesis 11:7** | נֵרְדָה "let us go down" | "Let us go down and confuse..." | Babel - Trinity or divine council? |
| **Isaiah 6:8** | עָנִינוּ "for us" | "Who will go for us?" | Mixed singular ("I") + plural ("us") = Trinity |

**Pattern**: First person plural in divine speech - Christian interpretation = Trinity (inclusive of three persons), alternative = divine council (God + angels, rejected by Christian orthodoxy)

### 4.2 New Testament - Epistolary "We"

**Epistles with co-authors** (ambiguous "we"):

| Epistle | Co-author(s) | Clusivity Challenge |
|---------|--------------|---------------------|
| 1 Corinthians | Sosthenes | "We" = Paul alone? Paul + Sosthenes? Paul + readers? |
| 2 Corinthians | Timothy | "We" varies by context |
| Philippians | Timothy | Opening "we" vs. later "I" |
| Colossians | Timothy | Mixed "we" and "I" |
| 1 Thessalonians | Silvanus + Timothy | Multiple co-authors - complex "we" |
| 2 Thessalonians | Silvanus + Timothy | Same complexity |
| Philemon | Timothy | Personal letter, occasional "we" |

**Pattern**: Epistolary "we" requires **verse-by-verse analysis** - no single answer for entire letter

### 4.3 New Testament - Prayers and Exhortations

| Passage | Greek | Context | Inclusive/Exclusive? |
|---------|-------|---------|---------------------|
| **Matthew 6:9-13** | "Our Father..." | Lord's Prayer | Exclusive (excluding God) {sil-1999-clusivity-nt} |
| **Luke 11:2-4** | "Our Father..." | Shorter Lord's Prayer | Exclusive (excluding God) |
| **Hebrews 4:14-16** | "Let us hold fast..." | Exhortation to readers | Inclusive (author + readers) |
| **Hebrews 10:22-24** | "Let us draw near..." | Exhortation | Inclusive (author + readers) |

**Pattern**: Prayers addressed to God = exclusive (God not in "our/us/we"); exhortations to believers = inclusive (author + readers together)

### 4.4 Gospels - Disciples Speaking

| Passage | Context | Inclusive/Exclusive? |
|---------|---------|---------------------|
| **Luke 9:33** | Peter: "Lord, it is good for us to be here" | DEBATE: Exclusive (excluding Jesus) OR Inclusive (including Jesus)? {sil-1999-clusivity-nt} |
| **Mark 9:38** / Luke 9:49 | "We saw someone casting out demons..." | Disciples reporting to Jesus - likely exclusive |
| **John 6:68** | Peter: "Lord, to whom shall we go?" | Disciples' dilemma - inclusive (all disciples) or exclusive (excluding Jesus)? |

**Pattern**: Disciples addressing Jesus - context determines whether Jesus is included in "we/us/our"

### 4.5 Acts - Apostolic Pronouncements

| Passage | Context | Inclusive/Exclusive? |
|---------|---------|---------------------|
| **Acts 15:25** | Jerusalem Council letter | **Exclusive** (apostles, not congregation) {tbta-source} |
| **Acts 15:28** | "It seemed good to the Holy Spirit and to us" | **Exclusive** (apostles + Holy Spirit, not congregation) |
| **Acts 21:18** | Paul + elders in Jerusalem | "We went to see James" - Paul + companions, exclusive |

**Pattern**: Apostolic decision-making = exclusive (leaders, not entire congregation)
