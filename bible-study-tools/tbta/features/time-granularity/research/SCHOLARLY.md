# Research: Time Granularity

## Executive Summary

Time granularity captures the level of temporal precision and specificity in biblical texts, ranging from general temporal references ("in ancient times") to highly specific ones ("at the sixth hour"). This research synthesizes linguistic typology, biblical scholarship, and translation theory to understand how temporal expressions function cross-linguistically and the theological implications for Bible translation.

### Top 10 Findings

1. **Tense vs. Aspect Distinction**: Biblical Hebrew operates through an aspect-prominent system (perfective/imperfective) rather than tense, while Koine Greek encodes both aspect and tense primarily in the indicative mood. Temporal reference emerges from context rather than morphology in Hebrew {cook-2012-time}.

2. **Time Reckoning Systems**: First-century Jewish time reckoning counted twelve hours from sunrise (relative/seasonal hours), while Roman practice began at midnight. This dual system creates apparent contradictions in Gospel crucifixion accounts (Mark 15:25 vs. John 19:14) {crivoice-crucifixion}.

3. **Remoteness Distinctions**: Approximately 20% of the world's languages grammatically mark temporal distance (metrical/graded tense), with Bantu languages exhibiting up to 7 distinct past tenses based on hodiernal (today), hesternal (yesterday), and pre-hesternal categories {springer-graded-tense}.

4. **Event-Based vs. Clock-Based Time**: Indigenous Amazonian cultures (Huni Kuĩ, Awetý, Kamaiurá) employ event-based time intervals defined by natural/social cycles rather than metric calendar/clock units, creating fundamental translation challenges {frontiers-event-time}.

5. **Prophetic Year Precision**: Daniel 9's "seventy weeks" uses Hebrew *shavuim* (periods of seven), interpreted as 490 years using 360-day prophetic years based on biblical evidence (Genesis 7:11-8:4, Revelation 11:3, 12:6) {gotquestions-seventy-weeks}.

6. **Temporal Adverbials Typology**: Haspelmath's 50-language sample identified systematic types of time adverbials with cross-linguistic patterns in how languages encode temporal location, extent, frequency, and miscellaneous functions {haspelmath-1997-space-time}.

7. **Hebrew Temporal Idioms**: "Three days and three nights" (Matthew 12:40) represents Hebrew inclusive reckoning where any part of three days suffices, supported by multiple OT examples (Esther 4:16-5:1, Genesis 42:17-18, 1 Samuel 30) {1517-three-days}.

8. **Reichenbach's Reference Point**: Modern temporal semantics builds on Reichenbach's (1947) framework using three coordinates—Speech time (S), Event time (E), and Reference time (R)—to describe tense relationships, extended by Klein (1994) and Comrie (1985) {glottopedia-reichenbach}.

9. **Grammaticalization Pathways**: Bybee, Perkins & Pagliuca (1994) demonstrated that tense-aspect-modality categories evolve gradually through metaphorical extension and implicature conventionalization, following unidirectional paths across unrelated languages {uchicago-evolution-grammar}.

10. **Biblical Chronogenealogies**: Genesis 5 and 11 genealogies are unique in ancient Near Eastern literature for including father's age at son's birth, creating "chronogenealogies" with interlocking features suggesting completeness rather than gaps, though textual variants exist across Masoretic, Septuagint, and Samaritan traditions {biblearchaeology-genesis-chronology}.

## 3. Translation Case Studies

### Case Study 1: Crucifixion Timing - Hebrew/Greek Time Reckoning Systems

#### Background

The Gospel accounts of Jesus's crucifixion present an apparent temporal contradiction that exemplifies translation challenges with time granularity.

#### The Apparent Discrepancy

- **Mark 15:25**: "It was the third hour when they crucified him"
- **John 19:14**: "It was about the sixth hour" (at Pilate's final trial, before crucifixion)

#### Time Reckoning Systems in First-Century Judaea

**Jewish Time Reckoning** {crivoice-crucifixion}:
- Day begins at sundown
- 12 hours counted from sunrise (seasonal/relative hours)
- "Third hour" = approximately 9:00 AM (range: 7:30-10:00 AM)
- "Sixth hour" = approximately 12:00 noon (range: 10:30 AM-1:30 PM)
- Night divided into four watches: evening, midnight, cock-crow, morning

**Roman Time Reckoning** {crivoice-crucifixion}:
- Day begins at midnight
- Fixed hours counted from midnight
- Some scholars propose John used Roman system

#### Harmonization Attempts

1. **Roman vs. Jewish Time**: John may use Roman reckoning (midnight start), Mark uses Jewish (sunrise start)
2. **Approximate Times**: Ancient timekeeping lacked precision; "third hour" and "sixth hour" had overlapping ranges
3. **Inclusive Reckoning**: "Third hour" may include preparatory events leading to crucifixion
4. **Textual Variants**: Possible confusion of Greek numerals γ (3) and ϛ (6) in manuscript transmission

#### Translation Implications

**High Granularity Target Languages** (e.g., English):
- Translators must choose: "third hour," "9 AM," "about 9 o'clock," "mid-morning"
- Footnotes often explain Jewish time reckoning system
- Trade-off between precision and cultural authenticity

**Low Granularity Target Languages** (e.g., languages with only "morning/afternoon/evening"):
- Less problematic: "in the morning" suffices
- Loses precision but avoids apparent contradiction
- May require translator notes for theological study

**Event-Based Time Languages**:
- May render as "after sunrise" or "when sun was rising high"
- Natural fit with ancient context
- Challenges arise with prophecy fulfillment requiring precision

#### Key Findings

- **Time Granularity Affects Doctrine**: Crucifixion timing relates to Passover lamb sacrifice timing (theological significance)
- **Cultural Context Essential**: Translation decisions require understanding source culture's time system
- **Target Language Determines Strategy**: Language's inherent time granularity constrains translation choices
- **Footnotes vs. Text**: Balance between readable translation and preserving source precision

### Case Study 2: "Three Days and Three Nights" - Hebrew Temporal Idioms

#### Background

Matthew 12:40 records Jesus saying: "For as Jonah was three days and three nights in the belly of the great fish, so will the Son of Man be three days and three nights in the heart of the earth."

#### The Translation Challenge

Jesus was crucified Friday afternoon and rose Sunday morning—conventionally described as "three days" but only spanning two nights (Friday night, Saturday night). This creates apparent contradiction with "three days and three nights."

#### Hebrew Inclusive Reckoning

**Biblical Evidence** {1517-three-days}:

1. **Esther 4:16-5:1**:
   - Esther: "Fast for me, do not eat or drink for three days, night or day"
   - Esther 5:1: "On the third day Esther put on her royal robes"
   - Result: "Three days and three nights" = third day (parts of three days)

2. **Genesis 42:17-18**:
   - Joseph "put them all in custody for three days"
   - "On the third day, Joseph said to them..."
   - Three days includes arrival day, full day, and third day

3. **1 Samuel 30:12-13**:
   - Egyptian slave "had not eaten bread or drunk water for three days and three nights"
   - "My master abandoned me three days ago"
   - "Three days and three nights" = three days ago (inclusive)

#### Scholarly Interpretation

**Idiomatic View** {1517-three-days}:
- R.T. France: "Three days and three nights was a Jewish idiom appropriate to a period covering only two nights"
- Any part of a day counted as a full day in Hebrew reckoning
- Common Semitic idiom where part stands for whole

**Literalist View** {therain-three-days}:
- When both "days" and "nights" specified, expression becomes literal not idiomatic
- Requires full 72-hour period
- Necessitates Wednesday or Thursday crucifixion

#### Translation Implications

**Literal Translation Approach**:
- Translates word-for-word: "three days and three nights"
- Requires extensive footnotes explaining Hebrew idiom
- Risk of confusing readers unfamiliar with ancient reckoning

**Functional Equivalence Approach**:
- May render as "three days" (omitting nights) in languages without inclusive reckoning
- Adds explanatory phrase: "according to Hebrew reckoning" or "parts of three days"
- Loses some specificity but gains clarity

**Target Language Considerations**:

*Languages with Inclusive Reckoning*:
- Many non-Western languages have similar idioms
- Translates naturally without modification
- Examples: Some African, Asian, Pacific languages

*Languages with Exclusive Reckoning*:
- Western languages typically count full 24-hour periods
- Requires footnote or translator's note
- May need cultural bridge: "what we would call two days"

#### Key Findings

- **Cultural Distance**: Modern Western time concepts differ fundamentally from ancient Hebrew
- **Idiom Recognition**: Translators must identify idioms vs. literal statements
- **Multiple Biblical Examples**: Cross-referencing resolves apparent contradictions
- **Target Culture Matters**: Some cultures naturally understand inclusive reckoning, others don't

### Case Study 3: Time Systems in Papua New Guinea Bible Translation

#### Context

Papua New Guinea is the world's most linguistically diverse country with 800+ languages, 300 with no Scripture {frawley-2013-png}. Indigenous time systems differ fundamentally from biblical chronological time.

#### Indigenous Time Concepts in Bosavi (1975-1990)

**Research** {kulick-stroud-1990-bosavi}:
- Study of Kaluli language in Bosavi region
- Documents introduction of Western temporal concepts during missionization
- Shows linguistic and cultural transformation

**Pre-Contact Time System**:
- Event-based time intervals (not metric)
- Natural cycles: day/night, seasons, life events
- Social conventions: ceremonies, rituals, feasts
- No abstract chronological framework
- Time defined by events themselves, not external measure

**Post-Contact Changes**:
- New vocabulary in Kaluli and Tok Pisin for:
  - Days of week
  - Months
  - Years
  - Clock hours
- European institutional activities (church services, school, government) created need for scheduling
- Lexical innovations established authority: "This happened on Tuesday" vs. "This happened after the feast"
- Temporal markers became evidential (truth-claim) markers

#### Translation Challenges Identified

**Biblical Chronological Statements**:
- Genesis genealogies: "When Adam was 130 years old..."
- Prophetic timelines: "After 70 years I will restore..."
- Historical narrative: "In the 15th year of Tiberius Caesar..."

**Challenge**: Target languages lack:
1. Abstract counting of years
2. Calendar concepts
3. Age-based temporal reference
4. Historical chronology framework

**Solutions Employed**:
1. **Borrowed Terms**: Adopt Tok Pisin/English temporal vocabulary
2. **Descriptive Phrases**: "Many seasons passed" for "years"
3. **Event Sequences**: Chain events rather than date them
4. **Educational Materials**: Teach Western time concepts alongside translation
5. **Hybrid Systems**: Blend indigenous seasonal terms with borrowed calendar terms

#### Comparative Analysis: Amazonian Cultures

**Huni Kuĩ, Awetý, Kamaiurá** {sinha-etal-2011-event-time}:
- Similarly employ event-based (not metric) time
- Create hybrid calendars: 12-month cycle + indigenous seasonal indices
- Example: "Planting moon" (corresponds roughly to October)

#### Translation Principles Developed

**SIL/Wycliffe Approach**:
1. **Assess Temporal System**: Document target language's existing time expressions
2. **Identify Gaps**: Determine what biblical concepts lack equivalents
3. **Develop Strategy**:
   - **Option A**: Introduce new terms (borrowing or calques)
   - **Option B**: Use functional equivalents (event-based descriptions)
   - **Option C**: Hybrid approach (mix indigenous and borrowed terms)
4. **Consistency**: Create standardized glossary of temporal terms
5. **Teaching**: Provide helps for readers to understand biblical time system

#### Key Findings

- **Cultural Distance**: Greatest translation challenge is cultural, not merely linguistic
- **Time Granularity Varies**: From general (event-based) to specific (clock-based) across cultures
- **Innovation Required**: Translators must sometimes create new temporal vocabulary
- **Community Involvement**: Target community must adopt temporal framework for translation to succeed
- **Ongoing Process**: Time concepts continue evolving in minority language communities

#### Implications for Time Granularity Feature

1. **Not Universal**: Clock-based time granularity is Western construct, not universal
2. **Borrowing Common**: Many languages borrow temporal precision vocabulary
3. **Hybrid Solutions**: Most successful translations blend indigenous and introduced concepts
4. **Reader Education**: Footnotes, glossaries, teaching materials essential
5. **Prioritize Meaning**: Functional equivalence over formal equivalence for temporal expressions

## 4. Key Biblical Passages Requiring Temporal Precision

### Crucifixion Timing Passages

#### Mark 15:25
- **Text**: "It was the third hour when they crucified him"
- **Temporal Precision**: Specific hour (approx. 9 AM)
- **Theological Significance**: Coordinates with Passover lamb sacrifice timing
- **Translation Challenge**: Jewish vs. Roman time reckoning systems

#### John 19:14
- **Text**: "It was the day of Preparation of the Passover; it was about the sixth hour"
- **Temporal Precision**: Specific hour (approx. noon or 6 AM depending on system)
- **Theological Significance**: Timing of judgment, Passover preparation
- **Translation Challenge**: Harmonization with Synoptic accounts

#### Matthew 27:45-46
- **Text**: "Now from the sixth hour there was darkness over all the land until the ninth hour. And about the ninth hour Jesus cried out..."
- **Temporal Precision**: Three-hour period (noon-3 PM)
- **Theological Significance**: Supernatural darkness, duration of suffering
- **Translation Challenge**: Conveying precise duration in languages with general time concepts

### Resurrection Timing Passages

#### Matthew 12:40
- **Text**: "For just as Jonah was three days and three nights in the belly of the great fish, so will the Son of Man be three days and three nights in the heart of the earth"
- **Temporal Precision**: Idiomatic "three days and three nights"
- **Theological Significance**: Prophetic sign, resurrection prediction
- **Translation Challenge**: Inclusive vs. exclusive reckoning, Hebrew idiom

#### Matthew 28:1
- **Text**: "Now after the Sabbath, toward the dawn of the first day of the week..."
- **Temporal Precision**: Specific day and time of day
- **Theological Significance**: New creation theology (first day), post-Sabbath timing
- **Translation Challenge**: Sabbath-Sunday transition, calendar systems

#### John 20:1
- **Text**: "Now on the first day of the week Mary Magdalene came to the tomb early, while it was still dark"
- **Temporal Precision**: Very specific (early morning, pre-dawn)
- **Theological Significance**: Mary's devotion, empty tomb discovery timing
- **Translation Challenge**: Gradations of early morning (dark, twilight, dawn)

### Prophetic Timeline Passages

#### Daniel 9:24-27 (Seventy Weeks)
- **Text**: "Seventy weeks are decreed about your people and your holy city... Know therefore and understand that from the going out of the word to restore and build Jerusalem to the coming of an anointed one, a prince, there shall be seven weeks. Then for sixty-two weeks it shall be built again... And after the sixty-two weeks, an anointed one shall be cut off..."
- **Temporal Precision**: Highly specific prophetic periods (490 years using 360-day years)
- **Hebrew**: שָׁבֻעִים (shavu'im) = "sevens" (periods of seven)
- **Theological Significance**: Messianic prophecy, prophetic calendar, redemptive history
- **Translation Challenges**:
  - Literal "weeks" vs. "weeks of years"
  - 360-day prophetic year vs. 365-day solar year
  - Calculating specific dates (controversial)
  - Target language concepts of extended time periods

#### Daniel 12:7, 11-12
- **Text**: "...it would be for a time, times, and half a time... From the time that the regular burnt offering is taken away and the abomination that makes desolate is set up, there shall be 1,290 days. Blessed is he who waits and arrives at the 1,335 days."
- **Temporal Precision**: Cryptic but precise (3.5 years = 1,260 days, plus 30-day and 75-day extensions)
- **Theological Significance**: End times prophecy, tribulation period
- **Translation Challenge**: Symbolic vs. literal time periods, connection to Revelation

#### Revelation 11:2-3; 12:6, 14; 13:5
- **Text**: "They will trample the holy city for forty-two months" (11:2); "And the woman fled into the wilderness... for 1,260 days" (12:6); "a time, and times, and half a time" (12:14); "it was allowed to exercise authority for forty-two months" (13:5)
- **Temporal Precision**: 3.5 years expressed four ways (42 months, 1,260 days, "time, times, half a time")
- **Mathematical Consistency**: 42 months × 30 days = 1,260 days (360-day year)
- **Theological Significance**: Great Tribulation period, persecution duration
- **Translation Challenge**: Maintaining mathematical equivalence, symbolic vs. literal interpretation

### Genesis Chronogenealogies

#### Genesis 5 (Adam to Noah)
- **Example - Genesis 5:3**: "When Adam had lived 130 years, he fathered a son in his own likeness, after his image, and named him Seth"
- **Pattern**: Repeated for each patriarch with precise ages
- **Temporal Precision**: Exact years for father's age at son's birth, remaining years, total lifespan
- **Theological Significance**: Chronology from creation, human lifespan pre/post-flood
- **Translation Challenge**: Cultures without age-counting systems, large numbers

#### Genesis 11 (Shem to Abraham)
- **Example - Genesis 11:10**: "When Shem had lived 100 years, he fathered Arpachshad two years after the flood"
- **Temporal Precision**: Ages plus chronological anchors ("two years after flood")
- **Theological Significance**: Post-flood chronology, patriarchal lineage
- **Translation Challenge**: Historical vs. symbolic interpretation, textual variants (MT, LXX, SP differ)

### Exodus Chronology

#### Exodus 12:40-41
- **Text**: "The time that the people of Israel lived in Egypt was 430 years. At the end of 430 years, on that very day, all the hosts of the LORD went out from the land of Egypt."
- **Temporal Precision**: Exact duration to the day
- **Theological Significance**: Fulfillment of Genesis 15 prophecy, divine timing
- **Translation Challenge**: Long time periods, "on that very day" emphasizing precision

#### 1 Kings 6:1
- **Text**: "In the four hundred and eightieth year after the people of Israel came out of the land of Egypt, in the fourth year of Solomon's reign over Israel... he began to build the house of the LORD"
- **Temporal Precision**: Multiple chronological anchors (480 years from Exodus, 4th year of Solomon)
- **Theological Significance**: Temple timing, redemptive history milestones
- **Translation Challenge**: Connecting multiple time references, large numbers

### Jesus's Public Ministry Duration

#### John 2:13, 6:4, 11:55 (Passover References)
- **Text**: Three explicit Passover mentions during Jesus's ministry
- **Temporal Precision**: Minimum 2+ years of ministry (possibly 3+ years)
- **Theological Significance**: Ministry duration, festival fulfillment
- **Translation Challenge**: Tracking multiple Passover references across narrative

### Birth Narratives

#### Luke 2:1-7
- **Text**: "In those days a decree went out from Caesar Augustus... This was the first registration when Quirinius was governor of Syria... while they were there, the time came for her to give birth"
- **Temporal Precision**: Historical anchors (Caesar Augustus, Quirinius), event sequence
- **Theological Significance**: Historical verification, fulfillment of prophecy
- **Translation Challenge**: Historical references unfamiliar to target audience, vague "in those days"

#### Luke 3:1-2
- **Text**: "In the fifteenth year of the reign of Tiberius Caesar, Pontius Pilate being governor of Judea, and Herod being tetrarch of Galilee... the word of God came to John..."
- **Temporal Precision**: Multiple concurrent chronological anchors (six political figures)
- **Theological Significance**: Historical grounding, divine timing
- **Translation Challenge**: Complex political references, target language concepts of regnal years

### Passion Week Chronology

#### Mark 14:1 - 16:8 (Detailed Day-by-Day Account)
- **Sunday**: Triumphal Entry (Mark 11:1-11)
- **Monday**: Cleansing Temple (Mark 11:12-19)
- **Tuesday**: Teaching in Temple (Mark 11:20 - 13:37)
- **Wednesday**: Silence (no recorded events)
- **Thursday**: Passover preparation, Last Supper (Mark 14:12-31)
- **Friday**: Trial, crucifixion, burial (Mark 14:32 - 15:47)
- **Saturday**: Sabbath rest (no recorded events)
- **Sunday**: Resurrection (Mark 16:1-8)

**Temporal Precision**: Day-by-day sequence with specific activities
**Theological Significance**: Fulfillment of Passover typology, precise timing of atonement
**Translation Challenge**: Maintaining chronological clarity across multiple pericopes

### Gestation and Birth Timings

#### Luke 1:24-26, 36, 56-57
- **Text**: "After these days his wife Elizabeth conceived... In the sixth month the angel Gabriel was sent from God... And Mary remained with her about three months and returned to her home. Now the time came for Elizabeth to give birth"
- **Temporal Precision**: Pregnancy tracking (six months, three months, nine months)
- **Theological Significance**: Miraculous timing, coordination of John and Jesus births
- **Translation Challenge**: Pregnancy duration concepts, relative timing

- Daniel 9:24-27; Daniel 12:7-12; Revelation 11-13
- **Stakes**: Messianic prophecy, end times doctrine
- **Granularity**: Year, month, day precision with symbolic interpretation

**Genesis Chronogenealogies**:
- Genesis 5; Genesis 11
- **Stakes**: Creation chronology, flood dating, textual inerrancy debates
- **Granularity**: Year-level precision

### Medium Precision Required (Non-Arbitrary - Contextual)

**Historical Anchors**:
- Luke 2:1-7; Luke 3:1-2; 1 Kings 6:1
- **Stakes**: Historical reliability, contextual understanding
- **Granularity**: Regnal year, event sequence

- Mark 14-16 (parallels in other Gospels)
- **Stakes**: Narrative coherence, theological sequence
- **Granularity**: Day-by-day precision

**Ministry Duration**:
- John 2:13; 6:4; 11:55 (Passover references)
- **Stakes**: Ministry length, theological completeness
- **Granularity**: Year-level with festival markers

### Low Precision (Arbitrary - Stylistic)

**General Temporal Phrases**:
- "In those days" (Luke 2:1; Acts 1:15)
- "After these things" (Genesis 15:1; John 3:22)
- "Not many days later" (Luke 15:13)
- **Stakes**: Narrative flow (not doctrine)
- **Granularity**: Vague, context-dependent

**Estimated Time 85%+ of temporal references**:
- Most temporal expressions in narrative are stylistic, not theologically critical
- Examples: "Then Jesus said..." "Later..." "Soon afterward..."
