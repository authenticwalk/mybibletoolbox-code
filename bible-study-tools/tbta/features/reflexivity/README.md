# Reflexivity

Marks whether action affects subject itself (reflexive: "gave **himself**") or involves mutual action (reciprocal: "love **one another**").

## Quick Facts

| Aspect | Details |
|--------|---------|
| **Values** | `r` Reflexive, `R` Reciprocal, `N` Not applicable |
| **Source** | ✅ Greek middle voice, Hebrew Hitpael/Niphal |
| **Tier** | B (Important) |
| **Stakes** | HIGH: Christological self-sacrifice, mutual love |
| **Data** | 685 annotations: 398 Reflexive, 287 Reciprocal |

## Target Languages

**Mandatory marking**: Slavic (Belarusian), Romance (Spanish), Germanic (German), Semitic (Arabic)
**Optional marking**: East Asian (Chinese, Japanese), Austronesian

## Key Examples

### Galatians 2:20 "gave himself" - Reflexive (r)
**Stakes**: ⚠️ **CRITICAL** - Atonement theology
- Greek: παραδόντος ἑαυτόν (active + reflexive pronoun)
- Required: Reflexive marking to show voluntary self-sacrifice
- Forbidden: Non-reflexive (loses voluntary nature)

### John 13:34 "love one another" - Reciprocal (R)
**Stakes**: ⚠️ **HIGH** - Defining mark of believers
- Greek: ἀγαπᾶτε ἀλλήλους (reciprocal pronoun)
- Required: Reciprocal marking
- Forbidden: ❌ "love yourselves" (reflexive = theologically wrong)

### 1 Cor 13:8 "will cease" - Not Applicable (N)
- Greek: παύσονται (middle voice form)
- Deponent verb - middle form, active meaning
- Mark as N despite morphology

## Challenges

1. **Deponent verbs**: Greek middle forms with active meaning (mark N)
2. **Hebrew ambiguity**: Hitpael = reflexive OR reciprocal OR iterative
3. **Implicit reciprocals**: Plural middle without explicit pronoun
4. **Theological passages**: ~15 high-stakes contexts need 100% accuracy

## Research

**27+ sources**: Kemmer (1993), Allan (2003), Haspelmath (2008), Wallace (1996), WALS 47A/106A

**Key findings**:
- Source languages mark morphologically
- 85%+ arbitrary, but theological contexts non-arbitrary
- Reflexive-reciprocal polysemy NOT universal
- Semantic over morphological (deponents!)
