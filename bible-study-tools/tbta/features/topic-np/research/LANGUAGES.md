# Language Typology: Topic-Prominent vs Subject-Prominent

## Executive Summary

Topic-prominence is a fundamental typological distinction proposed by Li & Thompson (1976) that affects approximately 25-30% of world languages. **Neither Biblical Hebrew nor Koine Greek explicitly encodes topic-prominence morphologically**, though both use word order and information structure to mark topics pragmatically. This feature is primarily relevant for languages with grammaticalized topic marking (e.g., Japanese wa/ga, Korean eun/neun) or where topic-comment structure dominates syntax (e.g., Mandarin Chinese, Thai).

**Key Finding**: Of 1,009 translation varieties in our database, we have excellent coverage of topic-prominent languages (Japanese, Korean, Mandarin, Thai, Vietnamese, Indonesian, Tagalog, Cebuano, Ilocano) representing 4 major language families (Japonic, Koreanic, Sino-Tibetan, Kra-Dai, Austroasiatic, Austronesian).

## 1. Source Language Encoding (Hebrew & Greek)

### Biblical Hebrew

**Typological Classification**: Mixed (predominantly subject-prominent with topic-prominent features)

**Encoding**: Topic-prominence is **NOT morphologically encoded** in Hebrew. However:
- Hebrew uses **word order variation** to mark information structure
- Unmarked/neutral order: **VSO** (Verb-Subject-Object) in narrative contexts
- Topic-fronting: **SVO** or **XVO** (where X = topicalized constituent) is marked
- Information structure (given vs. new) influences constituent order more than strict syntax

**Source**: [Verb–Subject–Object (VSO) Word Order in Biblical Hebrew](https://biblicalhebrew.org/verb-subject-object-vso-word-order-in-biblical-hebrew-syntax-style-and-theology.aspx), [The drift from VSO to SVO in Biblical Hebrew: The pragmatics of tense-aspect](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/281497787_The_drift_from_VSO_to_SVO_in_Biblical_Hebrew_The_pragmatics_of_tense-aspect)

**TBTA Implication**: Topic NP annotations for Hebrew will be based on discourse-pragmatic analysis (participant tracking, given/new status), not morphological marking.

---

### Koine Greek

**Typological Classification**: Subject-prominent (with pragmatic topic-fronting)

**Encoding**: Topic-prominence is **NOT morphologically encoded** in Greek. However:
- Greek is **non-configurational**: word order is driven by information structure
- Preverbal position is pragmatically marked (topic or focus)
- Natural Information Flow: established information precedes new information
- Fronted constituents can be either **topical** (contextualizing) or **focal** (most salient)

**Source**: [A brief note on Greek word order](https://koine-greek.com/2023/08/18/a-brief-note-on-greek-word-order/), [Constituent Order and 'Emphasis' in the Greek New Testament](https://www.sil.org/system/files/reapdata/19/68/77/19687707412406048008441271071859355015/ConstitOrder.pdf)

**TBTA Implication**: Topic NP annotations for Greek will reflect discourse-pragmatic roles, not case marking or verb agreement.

## 2. Language Family Typology

### 2.1 Li & Thompson's Four-Way Typology (1976)

| Type | Definition | % of Languages | Examples |
|------|------------|----------------|----------|
| **Subject-prominent** | Subject is primary syntactic pivot; topic is marked/emphatic | ~60-65% | English, French, German, Spanish, Russian, Arabic |
| **Topic-prominent** | Topic is primary; subject may be implicit; topic-comment is unmarked | ~25-30% | Mandarin, Japanese, Korean, Thai, Vietnamese, Indonesian (suspected) |
| **Both** | Distinct subject AND topic systems (often with voice alternations) | ~10-15% | Tagalog, Ilocano, Cebuano (Austronesian focus systems) |
| **Neither** | No clear subject or topic (verb-prominent, polysynthetic) | <5% | Some Mayan languages (suspected), some Algonquian (suspected) |

**Source**: [Subject and Topic: A New Typology of Language (Li & Thompson 1976)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/244443556_Subject_and_Topic_A_New_Typology_of_Language), [Topic-prominent language - Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topic-prominent_language)

## 5. Typological Distinctions Between Languages

### 5.1 Topic Marking Strategies

| Strategy | Languages | Characteristics |
|----------|-----------|-----------------|
| **Grammatical particle** | Japanese (wa), Korean (eun/neun) - **Korean not in DB** | Obligatory choice between topic and subject markers; highly grammaticalized |
| **Positional (sentence-initial)** | Mandarin, Thai, Vietnamese, Indonesian | Topic = sentence-initial position; no overt marker; context-driven |
| **Marked particle (optional)** | Vietnamese (Còn...thì), Mandarin (的話 de huà) | Optional marking for contrastive/emphatic topics |
| **Voice alternation** | Tagalog (ang + voice), Indonesian (meN-/di-) | Topic selection triggers voice morphology on verb |
| **Emphatic fronting only** | English, Spanish, French, German | Topic-fronting is marked/literary ("The fish, I ate it"); subject-predicate is unmarked |

---

### 5.2 Pro-Drop and Topic Continuity

| Pro-Drop Level | Languages | Topic Encoding |
|----------------|-----------|----------------|
| **Extreme** | Thai, Mandarin | Topic routinely dropped after first mention; zero anaphora is default |
| **High** | Japanese, Korean, Vietnamese | Topic can be dropped when discourse-given; wa/eun can have zero realization |
| **Moderate** | Indonesian, Tagalog | Pro-drop occurs but less frequent; often retains pronouns |
| **Low/None** | English, Spanish, French, German | Subject pronouns usually required (though Spanish allows null subjects) |

**TBTA Integration**: Surface Realization feature (Zero, Pronoun, Full NP) should align with Topic NP (A/P/N) and Participant Tracking (New/Given/Routine/Restaging).

---

### 5.3 Information Structure Preferences

| Language Type | Given Information Encoding | New Information Encoding |
|---------------|---------------------------|--------------------------|
| **Topic-prominent** | Sentence-initial (topic position) or zero (pro-drop) | Post-verbal (focus position) |
| **Subject-prominent** | Subject position (may be initial or post-verbal) | Various (focus can be fronted for emphasis) |
| **Both (Austronesian)** | ang-marked NP (topic) | Non-ang arguments (ng, sa) |

**Translation Challenge**: Greek/Hebrew "given information" → Japanese/Korean/Mandarin "topic marker" vs English "subject position."

## 6. Cultural and Pragmatic Nuances

### 6.1 Honorifics and Topic (Japanese, Korean, Thai)

- **Japanese**: Topic choice (wa) interacts with honorific register. Omitting the topic (pro-drop) can be pragmatically marked for politeness or deference.
- **Korean (not in DB)**: Similar to Japanese; eun/neun interacts with speech levels (formal, informal, honorific).
- **Thai**: Extreme pro-drop related to social deixis; omitting the subject/topic conveys politeness or distance.

**TBTA Implication**: In honorific contexts (e.g., speech to/about God, Jesus, authorities), topic realization (overt wa vs zero) may have theological implications (suspected).

---

### 6.2 Definiteness and Topic (Chinese, Thai, Vietnamese)

- **Mandarin, Thai, Vietnamese**: No grammatical articles (no "the/a"). Topic-prominence correlates with **definiteness**: topics are typically definite or generic, not indefinite.
- **Translation Issue**: English "A man came" (indefinite subject) → Mandarin cannot front "a man" as topic. Must use presentational structure (e.g., "有一個人來了" yǒu yīgèrén láile "there-is one person came").

**TBTA Implication**: Topic NP = N (no topic) may correlate with indefinite/new participants in topic-prominent languages.

---

### 6.3 Passivization and Topic (Tagalog, Indonesian)

- **Tagalog**: Patient topic constructions (using patient voice -in) are **more common** than agent topic constructions, even when translating English active sentences.
  - English: "God created the world" (agent=subject)
  - Tagalog: "Nilalang ng Diyos ang langit at lupa" (patient=topic, using patient voice)
- **Indonesian**: Similar preference for di- passive when patient is topic.

**TBTA Implication**: Topic NP = P (patient topic) should trigger voice selection in Austronesian languages, not just word order.

---

### 6.4 Theological Sensitivity: Trinity and Topic (Japanese, Korean, Mandarin)

- **Genesis 1:26 "Let US make man in OUR image"**: In languages with trial number and topic-prominence, topic marking affects Trinitarian interpretation.
  - Topic = "We (trial)" → Topic marker (wa/eun) → encodes "exactly 3 persons speaking"
  - If topic is dropped (zero), does that obscure the Trinity? (suspected, requires theological review)
- **Japanese translation precedent**: "神は言われた「我々にかたどって…」" (Kami-wa iwareta "Wareware-ni katadotte...") → God (topic) said "in our image." Topic = God (singular) is maintained.

**TBTA Implication**: Topic NP = A (God as topic) must be carefully analyzed in Trinitarian contexts for topic-prominent languages.

## 7. Summary and Recommendations

### 7.1 Key Findings

1. **Source Languages**: Neither Hebrew nor Greek morphologically encode topic-prominence. Both use discourse-pragmatic strategies (word order, given/new).
2. **Typological Distribution**: ~25-30% of languages are topic-prominent (East Asian, Southeast Asian, some Austronesian). ~60-65% are subject-prominent (Indo-European, Afro-Asiatic, Niger-Congo).
3. **Database Coverage**: Excellent coverage of topic-prominent languages (Japanese, Mandarin, Thai, Vietnamese, Indonesian, Tagalog, Cebuano, Ilocano). **Critical gap: Korean** (major language, not in database).
4. **Austronesian Diversity**: 176 Austronesian translations; Philippine languages (Tagalog, etc.) have unique **Both** typology (topic + subject + voice). Requires special handling.
5. **Translation Strategy**: Topic NP annotation is critical for particle selection (Japanese wa/ga), voice selection (Tagalog, Indonesian), and pro-drop decisions (Thai, Mandarin).

---

### 7.2 Recommended Actions for Stage 2

1. **Prioritize 8 candidate languages** (English, Mandarin, Japanese, Thai, Tagalog, Indonesian, Spanish, Vietnamese) for Translation Database.
2. **Seek Korean translation** if possible (critical gap).
3. **Integrate with Surface Realization feature**: Topic NP (A/P/N) + Surface Realization (Zero/Pronoun/Full NP) → Determines pro-drop.
4. **Integrate with Voice feature** (if exists): Topic NP = P in Tagalog/Indonesian → Triggers patient voice (-in, di-).
5. **Test theological contexts**: Genesis 1:26 (Trinity), John 1:1 (Logos), Matthew 5:3-12 (Beatitudes) for topic-marking implications.

### 7.3 Open Questions (Requires Further Research)

1. **Burmese topic-prominence**: Burmese (mya, 2 translations) is Sino-Tibetan (like Mandarin) but SOV order. Is it topic-prominent? (suspected but unverified)
2. **Mayan languages**: 41 translations. Are they topic-prominent, subject-prominent, or "neither" (verb-prominent)? (unknown)
3. **PNG Austronesian**: 100+ translations from Papua New Guinea Austronesian languages. How many follow Tagalog-type focus systems vs Indonesian-type voice systems? (requires case-by-case analysis)
4. **Creole substrates**: 15 Creole translations. Do they inherit topic-prominence from substrate languages (e.g., Chinese Pidgin English)? (suspected but unverified)
5. **Korean availability**: Can we acquire Korean translation data? (critical for comprehensive typological coverage)
