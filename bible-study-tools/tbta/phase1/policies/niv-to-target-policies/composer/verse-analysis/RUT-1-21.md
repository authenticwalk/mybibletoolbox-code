# Ruth 1:21 Analysis

## Step 1: Source Texts

### NIV
> I went away full, but the LORD has brought me back empty. Why call me Naomi? The LORD has afflicted me; the Almighty has brought misfortune upon me."

### Target Format (Expected Pattern)
> I(Naomi) went away full, but the LORD brought me(Naomi) back empty. Why do you(those women) call me(Naomi) Naomi? The LORD has caused me(Naomi) to suffer. The Almighty has caused bad things to happen to me(Naomi)."

## Step 2: Differences Identified

| NIV | Target | Type |
|-----|--------|------|
| "I" | "I(Naomi)" | First person referent (R28) |
| "me" | "me(Naomi)" | First person referent (R28) |
| "has brought" | "brought" | Perfect tense simplification (R27/R3) |
| "Why call me" | "Why do you(those women) call me(Naomi)" | Implicit subject addition + referents |
| "has afflicted me" | "has caused me(Naomi) to suffer" | Idiom simplification (R23) |
| "has brought misfortune upon me" | "has caused bad things to happen to me(Naomi)" | Idiom simplification (R23) |
| Semicolon → period | Sentence split | R2 |

## Step 3: New Rules Needed

### R33: Implicit Subject Addition
- **Pattern**: Add implicit subjects in questions/commands
- **Example**: "Why call me" → "Why do you call me"
- **Reason**: Explicit subject for translation
- **Priority**: Medium
