## NIV → Targets Process (GPT)

Use this when converting **NIV** (or similar) into **targets.tabitha.bible / English / Unchurched Adults** style.

### Step 1 — Copy NIV and align with source

1. **Copy NIV text** for the verse.
2. **Align to source (Hebrew/Greek)** just enough to:
   - Disambiguate vague phrases (e.g., “the land” → Israel when context is clear).
   - Confirm people-group and geography labels (e.g., “Ephrathites”, “Moab”).
3. Keep meaning **identical**; do not add new theology.

### Step 2 — Fix participants and references (SKILL pronouns)

4. For each **new participant**, use a **full noun phrase** (name + role/location if helpful).
5. For the **next mention** in the same or next verse, prefer **“That [man/woman/family]”** instead of 3rd-person pronouns.
6. Replace key **possessive pronouns** with **named possessors** when it clarifies discourse:
   - “his wife” → “Elimelech’s wife”
   - “her sons” → “Naomi’s sons”

### Step 3 — Simplify clauses (SKILL clauses)

7. Split **complex sentences** into short clauses with **one main verb** each.
8. Turn stacked modifiers into **relative clauses**:
   - “from Bethlehem in Judah” → “from Bethlehem, which was in Judah.”
   - “Ephrathites” → “were in Ephrah’s clan.”
9. Normalize time frames:
   - “In the days when the judges ruled” → “When judges were ruling Israel.”

### Step 4 — Make geography, groups, and times explicit

10. Always label **place type** and nesting:
    - “in a **town** named [TOWN], which was in <<the **region** named>> [REGION].”
    - “to a **country** named [COUNTRY].”
11. Express **nationality/people-group** as relative clauses:
    - “Ruth, who was from Moab”
    - “Elimelech and Naomi were in Ephrah’s clan.”
12. Expand seasonal markers:
    - “at the beginning of barley harvest” → “during the time when people started harvesting barley.”

### Step 5 — Existence, possession, and causation

13. Use **existential “be”** for states:
    - “there was a famine in Israel.”
14. Use **“have / live with”** for simple possession/companionship:
    - “Naomi lived with her two sons.”
    - “Naomi didn’t have her sons or her husband.”
15. Make **causation explicit**, especially for God:
    - “The LORD took those things away from me.”
    - “The LORD Almighty caused many bad things to happen to me.”

### Step 6 — Make implicit information explicit (SKILL implicit)

16. When NIV depends on **background assumptions**, add short `<<implicit>>` clauses for:
    - **Bridging events**: `<<Naomi’s sons grew up and became men.>>`
    - **Hidden motives**: `<<I won’t be able to take care of you.>>`
    - **Hidden consequences**: `<<If you come with me, you also will become very sad.>>`
17. Use **footnotes** (not `<< >>`) for:
    - Historical roles (e.g., judges vs kings).
    - Name meanings and wordplays (Naomi/Mara, etc.).

### Step 7 — Speech, questions, blessings, and oaths

18. Keep **speaker and addressee explicit**:
    - “Naomi said to Ruth…”
19. Preserve **rhetorical questions** but ensure key logic is also available in plain form nearby (possibly in `<< >>`).
20. Normalize **blessing formulas**:
    - “May the LORD be kind to you…”
    - “May the LORD give new husbands to you so that you can rest.”
21. Normalize **oath formulas**:
    - “I swear to the LORD that…”
    - “If I don’t keep this promise, the LORD should punish me severely.”

### Step 8 — Emotions, group actions, and travel

22. Make strong **emotions and reactions** explicit:
    - “wept loudly”, “became very bitter”, “became excited”, “stopped arguing”.
23. Use clear **group entities**:
    - “that family”, “the people who were living there”, “the women who were living in Bethlehem.”
24. Always mark **travel** with origin, route, and destination:
    - “left the place where they were living and started walking on the road that led to Judah.”
    - “Naomi and Ruth continued traveling to Bethlehem.”

### Step 9 — Vocabulary and ontology (reuse SKILL vocabulary + checklist)

25. Prefer **simple, concrete verbs and nouns** that are ontology‑friendly (see `rules/vocabulary.md`).
26. Replace or unpack **opaque idioms and metaphors** with simple paraphrases, while keeping theological meaning:
    - “find rest in the home of another husband” → “give new husbands to you so that you can rest.”
    - “went away full” → “I had many good things.”
27. Use **digits for numbers** and obey TBTA/SKILL lexical constraints.
