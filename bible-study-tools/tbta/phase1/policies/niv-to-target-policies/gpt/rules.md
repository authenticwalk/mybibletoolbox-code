## NIV → Targets Rules (GPT, ordered by importance)

These rules extend TBTA **SKILL** rules. When a rule overlaps SKILL, **follow SKILL**, and treat this as a NIV-specific sharpening.

---

### R1. Participant clarity and tracking  *(extends SKILL pronouns)*

- **R1.1** Introduce every new participant with a **full NP** (name + role/location if needed).
- **R1.2** For near‑repeat mentions, use **“That [man/woman/family]”** instead of 3rd-person pronouns.
- **R1.3** Replace key **possessive pronouns** with **named possessors** when it clarifies (“his wife” → “Elimelech’s wife”).

---

### R2. Clause simplification and splitting  *(extends SKILL clauses)*

- **R2.1** Prefer **one main verb per clause**; split compound or stacked clauses.
- **R2.2** Convert stacked modifiers into **relative clauses** or extra sentences:
  - “from Bethlehem in Judah” → “from Bethlehem, which was in Judah.”
  - “Ephrathites” → “were in Ephrah’s clan.”
- **R2.3** Normalize time idioms:
  - “In the days when X [ruled]” → “When X was [ruling] [people].”

---

### R3. Geography, groups, and type labels

- **R3.1** Always mark **place type** and nesting:
  - “in a **town** named [TOWN], which was in <<the **region** named>> [REGION].”
  - “to a **country** named [COUNTRY].”
- **R3.2** Express **nationality/people-group** with simple patterns:
  - “Ruth, who was from Moab.”
  - “[Name] and [Name] were in [X]’s clan.”
- **R3.3** Expand seasonal shorthand:
  - “at the beginning of barley harvest” → “during the time when people started harvesting barley.”

---

### R4. Existence, possession, and causation

- **R4.1** Use **existential “be”** for states and events:
  - “there was a famine in Israel.”
- **R4.2** Use **“have / live with”** for simple possession/companionship:
  - “Naomi lived with her two sons.”
  - “Naomi didn’t have her sons or her husband.”
- **R4.3** Make **causation explicit**, especially when God is the agent:
  - “The LORD took those things away from me.”
  - “The LORD Almighty caused many bad things to happen to me.”

---

### R5. Implicit information and bridging  *(uses SKILL implicit)*

- **R5.1** When NIV relies on **unspoken events**, add short `<<regular implicit>>` clauses:
  - Growth: `<<Naomi’s sons grew up and became men.>>`
  - Motive: `<<I won’t be able to take care of you.>>`
  - Consequence: `<<If you come with me, you also will become very sad.>>`
- **R5.2** Keep `<< >>` **inside** the verse; reserve **footnotes** for:
  - Historical roles (e.g., judges vs kings).
  - Name meanings and other lexical notes.

---

### R6. Time and sequence

- **R6.1** Use clear **sequence markers**: Then / So / But to track flow.
- **R6.2** Unpack complex time phrases into clauses:
  - “When [X] was living in [place]…” rather than bare prepositional phrases.
- **R6.3** For seasonal or festival timing, connect to **visible activities**:
  - “during the time when people started harvesting barley.”

---

### R7. Family, clan, and nationality

- **R7.1** Use **apposition or relative clauses** for roles:
  - “Elimelech, who was Naomi’s husband.”
- **R7.2** Normalize clan/people-group labels to **clan/family** language:
  - “They were in Ephrah’s clan.”
- **R7.3** Use generic “your people / my people” for covenant/ethnic identity unless the text demands more detail.

---

### R8. Speech, questions, blessings, and oaths  *(extends SKILL quotes + special)*

- **R8.1** Keep **speaker and addressee** explicit in each speech turn.
- **R8.2** Preserve **rhetorical questions**, but ensure crucial reasoning is also available in explicit statements (possibly in `<< >>`).
- **R8.3** Normalize **blessing formulas**:
  - “May the LORD be kind to you…”
  - “May the LORD give new husbands to you so that you can rest.”
- **R8.4** Normalize **oath formulas**:
  - “I swear to the LORD that…”
  - “If I don’t keep this promise, the LORD should punish me severely.”

---

### R9. Emotional and reaction events

- **R9.1** Make strong **emotions** explicit:
  - “wept loudly”, “became very bitter”, “became excited.”
- **R9.2** Name **discourse moves**:
  - “Naomi stopped arguing with Ruth.”

---

### R10. Name meanings and wordplays

- **R10.1** Keep the **name change** or play in the verse:
  - “Call me ‘Mara’ because the LORD Almighty caused me to become very bitter.”
- **R10.2** Explain lexical meanings in a **footnote**:
  - “Naomi means ‘sweet’. But Mara means ‘bitter’.”

---

### R11. Group entities and households

- **R11.1** Use **group referents** to avoid repetition:
  - “that family”, “the people who were living there”, “the women who were living in Bethlehem.”
- **R11.2** When NIV lists multiple names in one clause, **split**:
  - “One son’s name was [A]. And the other son’s name was [B].”

---

### R12. Travel and location transitions

- **R12.1** Always mark **origin, route, and destination**:
  - “left the place where they were living and started walking on the road that led to Judah.”
- **R12.2** For returns, keep both **from** and **to** explicit:
  - “So Naomi returned from Moab… Naomi and Ruth arrived in Bethlehem…”

---

### R13. Metaphor and abstract concept unpacking

- **R13.1** For key abstractions tied to daily life, add simple paraphrases:
  - “rest” → “rest in the safety of a new husband” (or “so that you can rest” when the context is clear).
  - “went away full” → “I had many good things.”
- **R13.2** Keep theological content unchanged while replacing opaque idioms with **plain, literal statements** that a translator can carry into any language.
