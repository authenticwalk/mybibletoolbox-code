## Executive Summary

The TBTA system employs a sophisticated set of discourse markers and connectives to maintain narrative flow and semantic relationships across verses. These markers serve to:
- Connect sequential events ("And", "Then")
- Mark contrasts ("But")
- Indicate causation ("Therefore", "Because", "So")
- Signal temporal relationships ("After that", "At that time", "Later", "One day")
- Introduce unexpected events ("Suddenly")
- Show similarity ("Similarly")
- Mark sequence endpoints ("Finally")

## 1. SENTENCE-INITIAL DISCOURSE MARKERS

### 1.1 Additive/Sequential: "And"

**Function:** Most common marker for narrative continuation and clause linking

**Examples:**

1. **Genesis 1:4** - Sequential actions
   `"God saw the light. And God thought [that the light was good.] Then God separated the darkness from the light."`

2. **Genesis 1:5** - Multiple related events
   `"God called the light day. And God called the darkness night."`

3. **Philemon 1:2** - Parallel actions
   `"And we(Paul) are also writing this letter to the other people [who worship God in your(Philemon's) house]."`

4. **Philemon 1:3** - Continued prayer content
   `"And we(Paul) pray [that God and Jesus Christ will cause [you(Philemon) to be peaceful]]."`

5. **Jonah 1:4** - Consequence chain
   `"But Yahweh caused [a strong wind to blow on the Mediterranean Sea]. And that wind caused a very big storm."`

6. **Jonah 1:6** - Narrative progression
   `"The leader of the ship came to Jonah. And the leader said to Jonah..."`

7. **Acts 1:6** - Combined with question
   `"One day the apostles were with Jesus. And the apostles asked Jesus..."`

8. **Acts 1:19** - Information flow
   `"And all the people [who live in Jerusalem] heard [that Judas died]. So people named that field Akeldama."`

**Pattern:** "And" typically appears:
- Between clauses within a verse describing related actions
- At the start of a verse continuing the previous narrative thread
- In lists of sequential actions by the same or different agents

### 1.2 Temporal Sequential: "Then"

**Function:** Marks temporal progression; stronger sequential force than "And"

**Examples:**

1. **Genesis 1:3** - After creation state established
   `"Then God said..."`

2. **Genesis 1:4** - After observation
   `"God saw the light. And God thought [that the light was good.] Then God separated the darkness from the light."`

3. **Genesis 1:6** - Next creation day
   `"Then God said..."`

4. **Jonah 1:7** - Response to crisis
   `"Then the men [who were working on the ship] said to each-other(men)..."`

5. **Jonah 1:8** - Follow-up action
   `"Then those men asked Jonah..."`

6. **Jonah 1:9** - Response in dialogue
   `"Then Jonah answered those men..."`

7. **Jonah 1:10** - Reaction sequence
   `"Those men found out [that Jonah was run-away from Yahweh]. So those men became very afraid. Then those men asked Jonah..."`

8. **Jonah 1:14** - After failed attempt
   `"Then the men cried out to Yahweh. And those men said..."`

9. **Jonah 1:15** - Critical action
   `"Then the men picked up Jonah. And those men threw Jonah into the water. Then the wind stopped blowing."`

10. **Acts 1:3** - Post-resurrection events
    `"[After Jesus died] God raised Jesus. Then Jesus showed the apostles [that Jesus was alive]."`

11. **Acts 1:12** - New scene
    `"Then the apostles returned from the Mountain named Olives to Jerusalem."`

12. **Acts 1:15** - Time progression
    `"After a few days all the people [who believed in Jesus] met. About 120 people came to this meeting. Then Peter stood up."`

**Pattern:** "Then" appears:
- At major narrative transitions
- After temporal or conditional clauses
- When indicating the next step in a sequence
- Often after another event is completed or observed

### 1.3 Contrastive: "But"

**Function:** Marks contrast, opposition, or unexpected development

**Examples:**

1. **Jonah 1:3** - Opposition to command
   `"But Jonah run-away from Yahweh. Jonah decided [to go to Tarshish]."`

2. **Jonah 1:4** - Divine intervention
   `"But Yahweh caused [a strong wind to blow on the Mediterranean Sea]."`

3. **Jonah 1:13** - Failed attempt
   `"But the men tried [to move/row/steer the ship to the land/shore]. But those men were not able [to move/row/steer the ship]..."`

4. **Jonah 2:6** - Rescue after despair
   `"I(Jonah) thought [that I(Jonah) will be in this grave forever]. But you(Yahweh) are Yahweh. You(Yahweh) are my(Jonah's) God."`

5. **Jonah 2:9** - Contrast with idolaters
   `"But I(Jonah) will give a gift/sacrifice to you(Yahweh). I(Jonah) will praise you(Yahweh) with a loud voice."`

6. **Philemon 1:9** - Unexpected approach
   `"But we(Paul) love each-other(Paul). And I(Paul) am now old."`

7. **Acts 1:5** - Contrast between baptisms
   `"John baptized people with water. But you(apostles) will be baptized with the Holy Spirit by God after a few days."`

8. **Acts 1:8** - Contrast with previous concern
   `"But the Holy Spirit will come to you(apostles). And the Holy Spirit will give power to you(apostles)."`

9. **Matthew 7:25** - House stands despite storm
   `"Then the rain fell-A. The rivers became deep. And the winds blew-A much on that house. But that house did not fall..."`

10. **Matthew 7:14** - Contrast with wide gate
    `"But the gate [that leads to life [(implicit-situational) that never ends]] is narrow."`

**Pattern:** "But" appears:
- When actions contradict expectations or previous statements
- To mark divine intervention against human plans
- In contrasting pairs of behaviors or outcomes
- To introduce unexpected resilience or change

### 1.4 Resultative: "So"

**Function:** Marks logical consequence or result

**Examples:**

1. **Jonah 1:3** - Decision leading to action
   `"Jonah decided [to go to Tarshish]. So Jonah went to Joppa."`

2. **Jonah 1:10** - Fear resulting from discovery
   `"Those men found out [that Jonah was run-away from Yahweh]. So those men became very afraid."`

3. **Jonah 1:11** - Question prompted by worsening situation
   `"The wind was blowing harder. And the waves were becoming bigger. So the men asked Jonah..."`

4. **Philemon 1:9** - Conclusion from circumstances
   `"So I(Paul) will ask [you(Philemon) to do this thing]."`

5. **Acts 1:19** - Naming resulting from event
   `"And all the people [who live in Jerusalem] heard [that Judas died]. So people named that field Akeldama."`

6. **Acts 1:21** - Action required by circumstance
   `"So now another man must join us(apostles)."`

7. **Acts 1:23** - Response to requirement
   `"So the apostles told two men [to stand in front of the people]."`

8. **Matthew 6:2** - Application of principle
   `"So [whenever you(person) give money or other things to poor people..."`

9. **Matthew 7:12** - Golden rule conclusion
   `"So you(people) (imp) always treat other people [just-like you(people) want [those people to treat you(people)]]."`

**Pattern:** "So" appears:
- After causal statements or circumstances
- To introduce logical conclusions
- When showing practical applications
- In decision-making sequences

## 2. TEMPORAL MARKERS

### 2.1 Specific Time Reference: "At that time"

**Examples:**

1. **1 Samuel 3:7** - Historical context
   `"At that time Samuel did not know Yahweh. Yahweh did not speak to Samuel previously."`

2. **1 Samuel 4:15** - Character description
   `"At that time Eli was 98 years old. And Eli was not able [to see things]."`

3. **1 Samuel 11:5** - Scene setting
   `"At that time Saul was plowing the fields with Saul's oxen."`

4. **1 Samuel 21:7** - Background information
   `"At that time one of Saul's servants was at that place."`

5. **1 Samuel 25:4** - Circumstantial setting
   `"At that time David was in the desert. And David heard [that Nabal was cutting Nabal's sheep's hair/wool]."`

**Pattern:** "At that time" appears:
- To establish simultaneous background conditions
- For contextual information about characters or situations
- When describing the state of affairs during narrative events

### 2.2 Sequential Time: "After that"

**Example:**

1. **Esther 1:5** - Event sequence
   `"After that party Xerxes invited all the people [that lived in Susa] [to come to another party]."`

**Pattern:** "After that" appears:
- To mark clear temporal sequence between events
- When one event is completed before the next begins

### 2.3 Subsequent Time: "Later"

**Examples:**

1. **1 Samuel 10:14** - Time gap
   `"Later Saul's uncle asked Saul and Saul's servant..."`

2. **1 Samuel 19:8** - Resume after time passage
   `"Later the Philistines started attacking the Israelites again."`

3. **1 Samuel 23:25** - New development
   `"Later Saul and Saul's soldiers started searching for David."`

4. **2 Samuel 11:26** - Following event
   `"Later Bathsheba heard[that Bathsheba's husband named Uriah was dead]."`

5. **2 Samuel 2:12** - Time transition
   `"Later Abner [who was the son of Ner] left Mahanaim. And Abner went to Gibeon."`

**Pattern:** "Later" appears:
- When unspecified time has passed
- To resume narrative after temporal gap
- For new developments in ongoing situations

### 2.4 Indefinite Past: "One day"

**Examples:**

1. **Jonah 1:1** - Story opening
   `"One day Yahweh spoke to Jonah."`

2. **Acts 1:4** - Specific but indefinite occasion
   `"One day [while Jesus was eating with the apostles] Jesus told [the apostles to stay in Jerusalem]."`

3. **Acts 1:6** - Scene marker
   `"One day the apostles were with Jesus. And the apostles asked Jesus..."`

**Pattern:** "One day" appears:
- To mark story beginnings or new episodes
- When the specific date is unimportant but the event is
- To introduce significant conversations or events

### 2.5 Temporal Subordinate Clauses: "[After X]", "[While X]", "[When X]"

**Examples:**

1. **Genesis 1:2** - Background sequence
   `"[After God had created the earth] no thing was on the earth."`

2. **Jonah 1:16** - Reaction to event
   `"[After the men saw those things] the men feared Yahweh greatly."`

3. **Jonah 2:1** - Circumstantial
   `"[While Jonah was inside the fish..."`

4. **Acts 1:3** - Time frame
   `"[After Jesus died] God raised Jesus. Then Jesus showed the apostles [that Jesus was alive]."`

5. **Acts 1:4** - Simultaneous action
   `"One day [while Jesus was eating with the apostles] Jesus told [the apostles to stay in Jerusalem]."`

6. **Acts 1:9** - Concurrent observation
   `"[While the apostles watched [Jesus being lifted by God]] Jesus went into a cloud."`

7. **Matthew 5:1** - Trigger for action
   `"[When Jesus saw the crowds [(implicit-situational) that were following Jesus]..."`

**Pattern:** Bracketed temporal clauses appear:
- To establish the timing or circumstances of the main clause
- For background information
- To show simultaneity or causation

## 3. CAUSAL MARKERS

### 3.1 Explicit Causation: "Because"

**Examples:**

1. **Jonah 1:13** - Reason for failure (in bracket)
   `"But those men were not able [to move/row/steer the ship] [because the wind was blowing harder] [and because the waves were becoming bigger]."`

2. **Philemon 1:5** - Reason for thanksgiving
   `"I(Paul) thank God [because I(Paul) heard good news [that is about you(Philemon)]]."`

3. **Philemon 1:8** - Reason for authority (in bracket)
   `"[Because Christ has given authority to me(Paul)] I(Paul) am able [to command [you(Philemon) to do this thing]]."`

4. **Matthew 7:25** - Reason for stability
   `"But that house did not fall [because that person built that house on rock]."`

**Pattern:** "Because" appears:
- Often within brackets as subordinate reason clauses
- To explain motivations or causes
- Can appear mid-sentence or in embedded clauses

### 3.2 Resultative Causation: "Therefore"

**Examples:**

1. **1 Samuel 2:30** - Divine declaration
   `"Therefore Israel's God named Yahweh says..."`

2. **2 John 1:8** - Exhortation based on warning
   `"Therefore you(people) should be careful [so that those people would not deceive you(people)]."`

3. **2 Samuel 12:10** - Judgment announcement
   `"Therefore your(David's) enemies will kill many of your(David's) descendants with swords."`

4. **2 Samuel 22:50** - Response to God's acts
   `"Therefore I(David) will praise you(Yahweh)."`

5. **Acts 3:19** - Call to action
   `"Therefore you(people) (imp) repent. You(people) (imp) return to God."`

6. **Acts 15:25** - Decision result
   `"Therefore we(leaders) agreed/decided [to choose some men] and [to send those men [to you(Gentiles)]]."`

7. **Daniel 4:27** - Counsel based on interpretation
   `"Therefore I(Daniel) suggest [that you(king) do certain things]."`

8. **Daniel 5:24** - Consequence
   `"Therefore God sent the hand [in order to write these words on the wall]."`

**Pattern:** "Therefore" appears:
- To introduce conclusions from previous arguments
- In prophetic judgments
- For applications of principles
- Often in formal or weighty declarations

### 3.3 Purpose/Result: "So that"

**Examples:**

1. **2 John 1:8** - Purpose of carefulness
   `"Therefore you(people) should be careful [so that those people would not deceive you(people)]."`

2. **Philemon 1:6** - Purpose of prayer
   `"I(Paul) pray [that these things will happen] [so that people would honor Christ]."`

3. **Matthew 7:1** - Purpose of not judging
   `"You(people) (imp) do not judge other people [so that you(people) would not be judged by God]."`

**Pattern:** "So that" appears:
- In brackets showing purpose or intended result
- Often with imperatives or exhortations
- Links action to desired outcome

## 4. UNEXPECTED EVENT MARKER: "Suddenly"

**Examples:**

1. **Acts 1:10** - Unexpected appearance
   `"The apostles were staring at the sky [that Jesus went to]. Suddenly two men [who were wearing white clothes] were standing beside the apostles."`

2. **Acts 16:26** - Divine intervention
   `"Suddenly..."`

3. **Daniel 5:5** - Miraculous event
   `"Suddenly the hand of a man appeared in the room. The hand wrote a message on the wall of the king's palace."`

4. **Mark 9:8** - Abrupt change
   `"Suddenly those followers/disciples saw [Jesus was now the only person [who was with those followers/disciples]]."`

5. **Matthew 8:24** - Crisis onset
   `"Suddenly there was a terrible storm on the lake [so-B waves started entering the boat]."`

6. **Matthew 28:2** - Dramatic event
   `"Suddenly the ground shook violently. For a servant/angel of the Lord-A came from heaven to the entrance of the hole/tomb."`

7. **Matthew 28:9** - Unexpected encounter
   `"Suddenly Jesus meet-D those women. Jesus said..."`

**Pattern:** "Suddenly" appears:
- For dramatic divine interventions
- At crisis moments
- When events happen without warning
- Often in resurrection/miracle narratives

## 5. SIMILARITY MARKER: "Similarly"

**Examples:**

1. **Jonah 4:11** - Comparison/application
   `"Similarly I(Yahweh) should feel pity for the great city named Nineveh."`

2. **Mark 13:29** - Analogical reasoning
   `"Similarly..."`

3. **Mark 3:26** - Parallel situation
   `"Similarly..."`

4. **Mark 15:31** - Parallel mockery
   `"Similarly the chief priests and the scribes were also laughing/mock at Jesus."`

**Pattern:** "Similarly" appears:
- To draw parallels between situations
- In teaching/application contexts
- When extending a principle to new cases

## 6. SEQUENTIAL ENDPOINT MARKERS

### 6.1 "Finally"

**Examples:**

1. **Matthew 21:37** - Last in sequence
   `"Finally that man sent-C that man's son to those people/farmers."`

2. **Matthew 22:27** - Concluding event
   `"Finally the woman died."`

**Pattern:** "Finally" appears:
- At the end of a series of attempts or events
- To mark the concluding action in a sequence

### 6.2 "First"

**Example:**

1. **Genesis 10:10** - Beginning of sequence
   `"First Nimrod built a kingdom [that was in Babylonia]."`

**Pattern:** "First" appears:
- To mark the initial action in a sequence
- When multiple stages are being described

## 7. DISCOURSE MARKER OMISSION PATTERNS

### 7.1 When "And" and Other Markers Are OMITTED

**Cases where verses begin WITHOUT discourse markers:**

1. **New sections/titles** - Marked with "(title)" or "-Title"
   - Genesis 1:1: `"God started creating the heavens and the earth."` (Story opening)
   - Jonah 1:1: `"Jonah (title) run-away from Yahweh."` (Section title)
   - Acts 1:12: `"-Title A new apostle is chosen by the apostles."`

2. **Direct speech/quotations**
   - Jonah 1:2: `"Yahweh said to Jonah..."` (Quote follows)
   - Acts 1:7: `"Jesus said to the apostles..."`

3. **Background/descriptive information**
   - Jonah 1:5: `"All of The men [who worked on that ship] were afraid."` (Scene description)
   - Acts 1:17: `"Judas served Jesus with us(apostles)."` (Background info)

4. **New character introduction**
   - Jonah 1:6: `"The leader of the ship came to Jonah."` (New agent)

5. **Parallel actions without connective**
   - Genesis 1:4: `"God saw the light. And God thought..."` (First clause has no marker)
   - Genesis 1:5: `"God called the light day."` (New verse, continuing same subject)

6. **Topic shifts**
   - Acts 1:22: `"That man must have been with us(apostles)..."` (Continuing requirements)
   - Matthew 7:3: `"(paragraph) (rhetorical) Why do you(person)..."` (New topic)

### 7.2 Clause-Initial vs. Sentence-Initial

**OBSERVATION:** Within a single verse:
- First clause often lacks discourse marker
- Subsequent clauses frequently use "And" or "Then" to connect

**Example from Genesis 1:4:**
```
"God saw the light. [NO MARKER]
And God thought [that the light was good.] [AND]
Then God separated the darkness from the light." [THEN]
```

**Example from Jonah 1:3:**
```
"But Jonah run-away from Yahweh. [BUT - contrasts with v2]
Jonah decided [to go to Tarshish]. [NO MARKER]
So Jonah went to Joppa. [SO - result]
And Jonah found a ship... [AND - continuation]"
```

## 8. NARRATIVE FLOW PATTERNS

### 8.1 Common Narrative Sequences

**Pattern 1: Action → Consequence → Response**
- Jonah 1:10: `"Those men found out [that Jonah was run-away from Yahweh]. So those men became very afraid. Then those men asked Jonah..."`

**Pattern 2: Command → Contrast → Result**
- Jonah 1:3: `"[Command in v2] But Jonah run-away from Yahweh. Jonah decided [to go to Tarshish]. So Jonah went to Joppa."`

**Pattern 3: Observation → Evaluation → Action**
- Genesis 1:4: `"God saw the light. And God thought [that the light was good.] Then God separated the darkness from the light."`

**Pattern 4: Crisis → Failed Attempt (But) → Desperate Action (Then)**
- Jonah 1:13-14: `"But the men tried [to move/row/steer the ship to the land/shore]. But those men were not able... Then the men cried out to Yahweh."`

**Pattern 5: Background ([When/While/After]) → Main Event**
- Acts 1:9: `"[After Jesus said these things] Jesus was lifted from the earth to the sky by God."`

### 8.2 Repetitive Patterns

**Genesis 1 Creation Pattern:**
1. `"Then God said..."` (Command initiation)
2. Action/Creation event (often no marker)
3. `"And God saw/thought..."` (Evaluation)
4. Time marker: `"God did these things on the Nth day."`

**Jonah Interrogation Pattern:**
1. `"Then the men asked Jonah..."` (Question)
2. Response (often new verse without marker or with "Then")
3. `"So the men..."` (Reaction)

## 9. CONCLUSIONS

### 9.1 Hierarchical Function of Markers

**Primary Narrative Continuity:**
- "And" - default continuation
- "Then" - temporal progression

**Logical/Semantic Relationships:**
- "But" - contrast/opposition
- "So" - immediate consequence
- "Therefore" - logical conclusion

**Temporal Specificity:**
- "One day" - indefinite past
- "At that time" - background conditions
- "Later" - time gap
- "After that" - immediate sequence
- "Finally" - sequence conclusion

**Event Quality:**
- "Suddenly" - unexpected/dramatic

**Comparative:**
- "Similarly" - parallel/analogous

### 9.2 Marker Distribution by Genre

**Narrative (Genesis, Jonah, Acts):**
- Heavy use of "Then", "And", "But"
- Temporal markers ("One day", "Later", "After that")
- "So" for plot causation
- "Suddenly" for dramatic events

**Epistolary (Philemon, 2 John):**
- "And" for thought continuity
- "Therefore" for logical argument
- "But" for contrast in reasoning
- "Because" for explanation

**Teaching (Matthew):**
- "So" for application
- "Therefore" for conclusion
- "But" for contrasting examples
- "For" (not heavily analyzed but present) for explanation

### 9.3 Translation Implications

**For Bible Translators:**
1. Discourse markers are NOT decorative - they carry semantic and structural meaning
2. "And" vs. "Then" distinction matters for temporal sequencing
3. Omission of markers signals new sections, topics, or direct speech
4. Marker choice affects perceived relationship between events
5. Some languages may need different markers or restructuring to convey the same discourse function

**Key Question for Translators:**
"Does my target language distinguish between additive continuation (And), temporal sequence (Then), and logical result (So)?"

### 9.4 CNL Design Observations

**Strengths:**
- Clear, consistent marker usage
- Temporal clauses in brackets maintain clarity
- Distinguishes between coordination types
- Preserves narrative flow across verse boundaries

**Features:**
- Discourse markers appear sentence-initially in most cases
- Markers are explicit rather than implied
- Temporal/causal subordination uses brackets consistently
- Agent tracking helps maintain reference across clauses
