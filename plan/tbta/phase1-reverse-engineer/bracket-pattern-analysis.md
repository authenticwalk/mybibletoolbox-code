# TBTA Verse Column: Bracket Usage Pattern Analysis

## Overview

The TBTA CNL (Controlled Natural Language) uses two types of brackets to make implicit information explicit:
- **[square brackets]** - for implicit/background information made explicit
- **(parentheses)** - for appositive/explanatory content and system markers

---

## SQUARE BRACKETS [ ] - IMPLICIT INFORMATION MADE EXPLICIT

Square brackets add information that is **implicit in the original text** but needs to be made **explicit for clarity** in the CNL transformation.

### 1. RELATIVE CLAUSES - Identifying/Clarifying Information

**Pattern**: `[which/who/that + verb phrase]`

Adds descriptive information to identify or clarify nouns.

**Examples:**

| Reference | Example | Trigger |
|-----------|---------|---------|
| Ruth 1:1 | "Bethlehem [which was in Judah]" | Location clarification |
| Ruth 1:2 | "a town named Bethlehem [which was in Judah]" | Geographic specificity |
| Ruth 1:3 | "Elimelech [who was Naomi's husband]" | Relationship clarification |
| 1 Sam 2:18 | "Samuel [who was a boy]" | Age/status clarification |
| 1 Sam 11:1 | "the city named Jabesh [which was in Gilead]" | Location specification |
| 1 Sam 17:1 | "The Philistines' army went to Sokoh [which was in Judah]" | Geographic context |
| 1 Sam 17:12 | "Jesse was living in Bethlehem [which was in the land [that the tribe named Judah owned]]" | Nested location info |
| 1 Sam 4:5 | "Yahweh was between the two angels [that were on the Ark-of-the-Covenant]" | Physical location |
| 1 Sam 1:9 | "the fork inside the pot. [When the servant pulled the fork out of the pot] the servant kept the meat [that was on that fork]" | Object specification |
| 1 Sam 13:5 | "The Philistines had 3000 chariots [that were from Moab]" | Origin specification |
| 2 John 1:1 | "you(people) [because we(John) know the true things [which God revealed]]" | Reason with nested relative |
| Acts 17:11 | "the people [who were from Berea] were eager to hear the message [which Paul preached]" | Multiple relative clauses |

**Trigger Rule**: When a noun needs additional identifying information that helps distinguish it from other similar entities or provides essential context.

---

### 2. TEMPORAL MARKERS - Time/Sequence Information

**Pattern**: `[When/After/Before/While/Whenever/until + clause]`

Makes explicit the **time relationship** between events.

#### 2a. Point-in-Time: [When...]

| Reference | Example | Trigger |
|-----------|---------|---------|
| Ruth 1:1 | "[When judges were ruling Israel] many people did not have enough food" | Background temporal setting |
| 1 Sam 8:1 | "[When Samuel became old] Samuel chose/appointed [Samuel's sons]" | Life stage timing |
| 1 Sam 10:11 | "[When Saul stood with the other people] Saul was much taller" | Simultaneous action |
| 1 Sam 4:5 | "[When those men brought the Ark-of-the-Covenant to the place [where the Israelites were camping]]" | Action timing with nested location |
| Ruth 1:6 | "[while Naomi was living in Moab] a person told Naomi" | Background ongoing state |

#### 2b. Sequential: [After...]

| Reference | Example | Trigger |
|-----------|---------|---------|
| Ruth 1:4 | "[after Naomi's sons married women], Naomi lived in Moab for ten years" | Sequence of events |
| 1 Sam 9:26 | "[After Samuel and Saul finished eating] Samuel and Saul left the place" | Event completion |
| 1 Sam 14:46 | "[After the Israelites finished chasing the Philistines] the Israelites returned" | Action completion |
| 1 Sam 1:24 | "[After Samuel was able [to eat food]] Hannah took Samuel to the tabernacle" | Milestone reached |
| 2 Sam 4:12 | "the soldiers buried Ish-Bosheth's head in Abner's tomb [which was at Hebron]" | Post-event location |

#### 2c. Prior Action: [Before...]

| Reference | Example | Trigger |
|-----------|---------|---------|
| 1 Sam 2:15 | "[Before a person burned the animal's fat] Eli's sons sent the sons' servant" | Prior action |
| 2 Sam 4:4 | "[Before Jonathan (implicit-info) died] Jonathan had a son named Mephibosheth" | Prior state |
| Acts 1:2 | "[Before Jesus went to heaven] Jesus talked to the apostles" | Prior to transition |

#### 2d. Simultaneous/During: [While...]

| Reference | Example | Trigger |
|-----------|---------|---------|
| Ruth 1:7 | "[While Saul and the servant were walking] Saul met some young women" | Ongoing simultaneous action |
| 1 Sam 17:34 | "Those men protected us(servants) [while we(servants) were in the fields]" | Duration of action |
| 2 Sam 3:30 | "Joab and Abishai killed Abner [while Abner and Asahel were fighting at Gibeon]" | Concurrent events |

#### 2e. Habitual/Repeated: [Whenever...]

| Reference | Example | Trigger |
|-----------|---------|---------|
| 1 Sam 1:7 | "[Whenever Hannah was at the tabernacle] Peninnah was cruel to Hannah" | Repeated occurrence |
| 1 Sam 2:13 | "[Whenever a person brought a sacrifice to Yahweh] that person boiled the meat" | Habitual practice |
| 1 Sam 17:34 | "[Whenever the Israelites saw Goliath] the Israelites run-away" | Regular response |
| 1 Sam 3:19 | "[Whenever Yahweh told Samuel [that a certain thing will happen]] that thing always happened" | Pattern of events |

#### 2f. Duration/Endpoint: [until...]

| Reference | Example | Trigger |
|-----------|---------|---------|
| 1 Sam 1:28 | "This child will belong to Yahweh [until this child dies]" | Duration endpoint |
| 1 Sam 3:14 | "I(Yahweh) will not stop punishing [until I(Yahweh) finish punishing]" | Action continuation limit |
| 1 Sam 10:8 | "you(Saul) will continue walking [until you(Saul) arrive at the big tree]" | Movement endpoint |

**Trigger Rule**: When the temporal relationship between events needs to be made explicit for proper understanding of sequence, simultaneity, or causation.

---

### 3. PURPOSE CLAUSES - Intent/Goal Information

**Pattern**: `[in order to + verb phrase]` or `[so that + clause]` or `[to + verb]`

Makes explicit the **purpose or intended result** of an action.

#### 3a. Purpose: [in order to...]

| Reference | Example | Trigger |
|-----------|---------|---------|
| Ruth 1:1 | "That man moved to a country named Moab [in order to live in Moab for a few years]" | Migration purpose |
| Ruth 1:6 | "Naomi prepared [to return to Bethlehem]" | Action goal |
| 1 Sam 1:3 | "Each year Elkanah went from Ramathaim to Shiloh [in order to worship Yahweh-Almighty]" | Travel purpose |
| 1 Sam 1:21 | "Elkanah went to Shiloh again [in order to give sacrifices to Yahweh]" | Visit intent |
| 1 Sam 9:12 | "The people will not start eating [until that man/seer arrives]" | Waiting reason |
| 1 Sam 7:6 | "the Philistines came to Mizpah [in order to attack the Israelites]" | Military intent |
| 1 Sam 13:2 | "Three groups left the camp [in order to attack the Israelites]" | Mission purpose |

#### 3b. Result: [so that...]

| Reference | Example | Trigger |
|-----------|---------|---------|
| Ruth 1:6 | "God had given food to those people [so that Naomi could live in Moab]" | Enabling result |
| 1 Sam 6:7 | "you(Philistines) (imp) put those baby cows in a pen [so that the baby cows could not follow]" | Preventing result |
| 1 Sam 6:8 | "You(Philistines) will give those gifts [so that the Israelites' god will forgive your(Philistines') sins]" | Intended outcome |
| 1 Sam 7:17 | "And those men took the Ark [so that God would accept Eleazar]" | Desired effect |
| 1 Sam 12:22 | "Yahweh will continue being with you(Israelites) [so that other people could know [that Yahweh's name is great]]" | Purpose with nested clause |

**Trigger Rule**: When an action has a stated or implied purpose/goal that needs to be made explicit for understanding motivation or causation.

---

### 4. CONDITIONAL CLAUSES - Hypothetical Situations

**Pattern**: `[If + condition] + result`

Makes explicit **conditional relationships** and hypothetical scenarios.

**Examples:**

| Reference | Example | Trigger |
|-----------|---------|---------|
| 1 Sam 12:14 | "[If you(Israelites) and your(Israelites') king obey Yahweh] Yahweh will protect you(Israelites)" | Conditional blessing |
| 1 Sam 2:25 | "[If one person sins against another person] God might help the person" | Hypothetical scenario |
| 1 Sam 6:9 | "[If the cows go toward Beth-Shemesh [which is in Israel]] Yahweh is punishing us(Philistines)" | Test condition |
| 1 Sam 14:10 | "[If the soldiers had eaten the food] the soldiers would have killed more Philistines" | Counterfactual |
| 1 Sam 17:36 | "[If the animal attacks me(David)] I(David) grab that animal's hair" | Conditional response |

**Trigger Rule**: When a statement presents a hypothetical condition and its potential consequence.

---

### 5. LOCATIVE/PLACE CLAUSES - Location Information

**Pattern**: `[where + clause]`

Makes explicit **location information** for events or states.

**Examples:**

| Reference | Example | Trigger |
|-----------|---------|---------|
| Ruth 1:7 | "Naomi left the place [that Naomi was living in]" | Previous location |
| 1 Sam 4:3 | "Yahweh-Almighty's Ark-of-the-Covenant to the place [where the Israelites were camping]" | Current location |
| 1 Sam 7:17 | "But Samuel always returned to Ramah [where Samuel's house was]" | Home location |
| 1 Sam 11:4 | "So messengers went from Jabesh to Gibeah [where Saul was living]" | Destination person location |
| 1 Sam 24:3 | "There was a cave near that place [where some sheep were in pens]" | Nearby location |
| Acts 17:21 | "Paul to a place [where people meet]. That place was named Areopagus" | Meeting place |

**Trigger Rule**: When location context is implicit but necessary for understanding where an event occurs or where people/things are situated.

---

### 6. REASON/CAUSE CLAUSES - Causal Information

**Pattern**: `[because + clause]`

Makes explicit the **reason or cause** for an action or state.

**Examples:**

| Reference | Example | Trigger |
|-----------|---------|---------|
| Ruth 1:8 | "[because you(daughters in law) cared for me(Naomi) and my(Naomi's) sons]" | Past action reason |
| 1 Sam 1:5 | "But Elkanah gave much meat to Hannah [because Elkanah loved Hannah much]" | Motivation |
| 1 Sam 1:7 | "Hannah did not eat food [because (implicit-info) Hannah was very sad]" | Emotional cause |
| 1 Sam 2:9 | "People do not defeat people's enemies [because the people are strong]" | Negated cause |
| 1 Sam 6:19 | "the people cried/mourned [because Yahweh punished those men]" | Response reason |
| 1 Sam 14:24 | "the Israelites run-away [because the Israelites were very afraid]" | Emotional cause |
| 1 Sam 18:22 | "Eli's sons did not listen to Eli [because Yahweh already decided [to kill Eli's sons]]" | Divine causation |

**Trigger Rule**: When a causal relationship needs to be made explicit to understand why an action occurred or a state exists.

## PARENTHESES ( ) - APPOSITIVE/EXPLANATORY CONTENT

Parentheses provide **supplementary information** that helps track referents, mark grammatical features, or clarify roles.

### 1. REFERENT TRACKING - Pronoun Clarification

**Pattern**: `(Name)` or `(noun phrase)`

Makes explicit **who or what** a pronoun refers to in the text.

**Examples:**

| Usage Pattern | Example | Count in Ruth | Count in 1 Sam |
|---------------|---------|---------------|----------------|
| Personal names | (Ruth), (Boaz), (Naomi) | 106, 53, 45 | (David) 390, (Saul) 336, (Samuel) 146 |
| Possessive forms | (Naomi's), (Ruth's), (Boaz's) | 12, 9, 9 | (David's) 50, (Saul's) 45 |
| Group referents | (people), (men), (women) | 15, 8, 3 | (people) 63, (Israelites) 136 |
| Role clarification | (daughters in law) | 14 | (soldiers) 18, (servants) 24 |

**Trigger Rule**: Whenever a pronoun (he, she, they, we, you, etc.) needs clarification about its referent for disambiguation.

---

### 2. SPECIAL SYSTEM MARKERS

**Pattern**: `(marker-name)`

Special tags that mark **grammatical, semantic, or structural features**.

#### 2a. (title) - Section Headers

| Reference | Example | Purpose |
|-----------|---------|---------|
| Ruth 1:1 | "Elimelech (title) and Naomi move from Bethlehem to Moab" | Marks section title/header |
| 1 Sam 2:12 | "(title) The sons of Eli are bad" | Content summary |
| 1 Sam 25:36 | "(title) Nabal dies" | Event preview |

**Count**: 4 in Ruth, 44 in 1 Samuel

#### 2b. (imp) - Imperative Mood

| Reference | Example | Purpose |
|-----------|---------|---------|
| Ruth 1:8 | "You(daughters in law) (imp) return to the houses" | Marks command |
| 1 Sam 3:9 | "So you(Samuel) (imp) go" | Marks directive |
| 1 Sam 6:7 | "Then you(Philistines) (imp) tie the wagon to those cows" | Marks instruction |

**Count**: 50 in Ruth, 268 in 1 Samuel

**Trigger Rule**: When a verb is in imperative mood (giving command/instruction).

#### 2c. (implicit-info) - Implied Information

| Reference | Example | Purpose |
|-----------|---------|---------|
| Ruth 2:1 | "Boaz (implicit-info) lived in Bethlehem. And (implicit-info) Boaz was rich" | Information inferred from context |
| 1 Sam 23:6 | "(Abiathar [who was the son of Ahimelek] ran/fled to David [who was at Keilah]. And Abiathar brought the special clothes [that priests wore] to David. David and Ahimelek (implicit-info) previously used those special clothes" | Past event inferred |
| 1 Sam 1:7 | "Hannah did not eat food [because (implicit-info) Hannah was very sad]" | Emotional state inferred |

**Count**: 12 in Ruth, 45 in 1 Samuel

**Trigger Rule**: When information is strongly implied by context but not explicitly stated in the text.

#### 2d. (vocab-alternate) - Vocabulary Alternatives

| Reference | Example | Purpose |
|-----------|---------|---------|
| 1 Sam 7:1 | "So those people consecrated (vocab-alternate) Eleazar" | Marks alternative word choice |

**Trigger Rule**: When a simpler or alternative vocabulary word can be used.

#### 2e. (footnote) - Additional Notes

**Count**: 2 in Ruth

### 3. ROLE/RELATIONSHIP CLARIFIERS

**Pattern**: `(role)` or `(relationship)`

Clarifies **social roles, relationships, or group membership**.

**Examples:**

| Type | Examples | Purpose |
|------|----------|---------|
| Family roles | (mother), (father), (husband), (wife) | Kinship clarification |
| Social roles | (leader), (servant), (soldier), (priest) | Status/position |
| Group membership | (Israelites), (Philistines), (people) | Ethnic/national identity |
| Ownership | (David's), (Saul's), (Naomi's) | Possessive relationships |

## NESTING PATTERNS

Brackets can be **nested** when multiple layers of implicit information need to be made explicit.

**Examples:**

| Reference | Nested Pattern | Example |
|-----------|----------------|---------|
| Ruth 1:1 | [relative [relative]] | "Bethlehem [which was in the land [that the tribe named Judah owned]]" |
| Ruth 1:6 | [relative [relative]] | "God's people [who were living in Israel [in order to serve God]]" |
| 1 Sam 10:2 | [location [relative]] | "Zelzah [which is near the border of the land [that the tribe named Benjamin owns]]" |
| 1 Sam 17:1 | [location [location]] | "Ephes-Dammim [which was between Sokoh and Azekah]" |
| 2 John 1:4 | [purpose [temporal]] | "[so that us(John) could live in peace] [while we(John) obey the true things [that God revealed]]" |

**Trigger Rule**: When clarifying information itself needs further clarification or when multiple implicit relationships stack.

## SUMMARY OF TRIGGERS

### Square Bracket [ ] Triggers:
1. **Noun needs identification** → [relative clause]
2. **Time relationship unclear** → [temporal marker]
3. **Purpose/goal unstated** → [purpose clause]
4. **Condition hypothetical** → [conditional clause]
5. **Location implicit** → [locative clause]
6. **Cause/reason unstated** → [reason clause]

### Parentheses ( ) Triggers:
1. **Pronoun referent unclear** → (name/noun)
2. **Grammatical mood needs marking** → (imp)
3. **Information strongly implied** → (implicit-info)
4. **Section boundary** → (title)
5. **Alternative vocabulary** → (vocab-alternate)
6. **Role/relationship needs clarification** → (role/relationship)

## DESIGN PRINCIPLES

1. **Explicitness over Ambiguity**: If information is implicit in the original but needed for understanding, add it in [square brackets]

2. **Referent Clarity**: Track all pronoun referents with (parentheses) to eliminate ambiguity

3. **Temporal Precision**: Make time relationships explicit with temporal markers

4. **Purpose Transparency**: State the purpose/goal of actions when not explicit

5. **Grammatical Marking**: Mark imperative mood and other grammatical features that affect meaning

6. **Nested Clarity**: Use nesting when clarifications themselves need clarification

This pattern system enables **unambiguous, explicit natural language** suitable for AI processing and translation work.
