## Rule 18: Clusivity Marking (_incl, _excl)

**Definition**: Marks inclusive "we" (includes addressee) vs exclusive "we" (excludes addressee)

### Best 5 Examples of _incl

   ```
   **Analysis**: Disciples preparing meal FOR "us(Jesus) _incl" = inclusive (includes Jesus)

   ```
   **Analysis**: "We(Jews) _incl" includes Jesus (who is also a Jew)

## Summary of Findings

| Rule | Marker | Examples Found | Contradictions Found | Consistency |
|------|--------|----------------|---------------------|-------------|
| 17 | _dual | Many (45+) | None | **Highly Consistent** |
| 18 | _incl/_excl | Many (40+) | Many (OT unmarked) | **Selective** (NT only?) |
| 19 | -iteration | 12 examples | None | **Consistent** (requires counts) |
| 20 | _1stAs3rd | Many (35+) | Some unmarked | **Mostly Consistent** |
| 21 | _reflexive | 13 examples | Many unmarked | **Selective** (conceptual only) |

### Key Insights

1. **_dual**: Most consistently applied rule - when exactly 2 entities are referenced together, they are marked
2. **_incl/_excl**: Applied in NT but not OT - suggests phased implementation or book-specific rules
3. **-iteration**: Requires explicit textual counts - not inferred from context
4. **_1stAs3rd**: Generally consistent but has capitalization variants (_1stAs3rd vs _1stas3rd)
5. **_reflexive**: Marks conceptual/emphasized reflexivity, not all grammatical reflexives

### Recommendations

1. Clarify whether _incl/_excl is NT-only or should be retrofitted to OT
2. Standardize capitalization for _1stAs3rd
3. Document criteria for _reflexive (conceptual vs. grammatical)
