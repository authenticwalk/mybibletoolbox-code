FOUR MAIN PATTERNS IDENTIFIED
================================================================================

1. PRONOUN(REFERENT) - DOMINANT PATTERN
   Frequency: 3,184+ occurrences
   Pattern:   pronoun(explicit-referent)
   Examples:  I(Paul), you(Philemon), he(God), we(John), your(people's)
   Usage:     First, second, and third person pronouns

2. REPEATED PROPER NAMES
   Frequency: 20+ clear examples in narrative books
   Pattern:   Name...Name...Name instead of Name...pronoun...pronoun
   Examples:  "Adam named...Adam named...Adam did not see...help Adam"
              vs KJV: "Adam gave names...for Adam there was not...for him"
   Usage:     Third-person narrative, especially Genesis

3. DEMONSTRATIVE + NOUN
   Frequency: 20+ clear examples
   Pattern:   this/that/these/those + noun-phrase
   Examples:  "these people" (← they), "this person" (← he/she),
              "that person" (← him/her), "those people" (← them)
   Usage:     Generic/class references, third-person plural

4. PRONOUNS RETAINED (Counter-examples)
   Frequency: 10 examples found
   Pattern:   Plain pronouns without annotation
   Examples:  "it was dark" (impersonal), "he was..." (narrative intro)
   Usage:     Impersonal constructions, immediately clear references

================================================================================
PRINCIPLE: ELIMINATE REFERENTIAL AMBIGUITY
================================================================================

TBTA makes ALL referential relationships explicit through:
  • Parenthetical annotation: pronoun(referent)
  • Lexical replacement: Repeated names or demonstrative+noun
  • Strategic retention: Only for impersonal/trivial references

PURPOSE: Enable unambiguous parsing for machine translation and Bible
         translation support systems.
