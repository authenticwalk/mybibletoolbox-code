# TBTA Comparison and Structure Rules - Evidence Analysis

Analysis of Rules 6, 26-31 from TBTA CNL transformation patterns.

## Rule 6: Named Introduction Formula - "a X named Y"

### BEST 5 EXAMPLES (First-mention introductions)

1. **1 Samuel 1:4** - "Elkanah's wife named Peninnah"
   - Pattern: `{noun} named {proper-name}`
   - Context: First introduction of Peninnah

2. **1 Samuel 1:9** - "The priest named Eli was at the tabernacle"
   - Pattern: `{role} named {proper-name}`
   - Context: First introduction of Eli with his role

3. **1 Samuel 14:50** - "A man named Abner was the leader/commander of Saul's army"
   - Pattern: `A {noun} named {proper-name}`
   - Context: First introduction with indefinite article

4. **1 Samuel 1:1** - "a town named Ramathaim... the tribe named Ephraim"
   - Pattern: Applied to both places and groups
   - Context: Geographic and tribal introductions

5. **1 Samuel 2:34** - "Your(Eli's) sons named Hophni and Phinehas will die"
   - Pattern: Works with multiple names
   - Context: Compound name introduction

### CONTRADICTIONS (Names introduced WITHOUT formula)

**Alternative Pattern: "The name of X was/is Y"**

1. **1 Samuel 8:2** - "The name of Samuel's first son was Joel. And the name of Samuel's second son was Abijah"
   - Uses: `The name of {noun} was {name}`
   - Not using: `named` formula

2. **Genesis 4:19** - "The name of Lamech's first wife was Adah. And the name of Lamech's second wife was Zillah"
   - Same alternative pattern for family introductions

3. **Esther 2:5** - "The name of Mordecai's father was Jair. And the name of Jair's father was Shimei"
   - Genealogical context prefers `name of X was Y`

**Analysis**: Two competing patterns exist:
- `X named Y` - for immediate, inline introductions
- `The name of X was Y` - for formal, genealogical introductions

## Rule 26: Simile Expansion - `[just-like X]`

### BEST 5 EXAMPLES (Clearest similes)

1. **1 Samuel 25:36** - "Nabal was having a feast [just-like kings have feasts]"
   - Full clause comparison with explicit action

2. **1 Samuel 8:5** - "We(Israelites) want a king [just-like the other nations have kings]"
   - Comparative desire/want construction

3. **Genesis 13:16** - "No person will be able [to count your descendants] [just-like no person is able [to count all the dust]]"
   - Complex nested comparison with ability statements

4. **Genesis 7:9** - "all kinds of animals entered the boat [just-like God promised Noah]"
   - Event/action matches promise

5. **1 Samuel 20:14** - "you(David) (imp) always be kind to me(Jonathan) [just-like Yahweh is kind to people]"
   - Character/attribute comparison

### CONTRADICTIONS (Similes NOT using just-like)

**Alternative Pattern: Simple "like"**

1. **1 Samuel 25:37** - "Nabal became like a stone"
   - Simple predicate: `became like {noun}`
   - Short, no clause expansion

2. **1 Samuel 2:2** - "No god is holy [like Yahweh is holy]"
   - Comparative adjective: `[like X is ADJ]`

3. **1 Samuel 17:43** - "Will you fight me with a stick [like people fight dogs]?"
   - Action comparison without `just-like`

4. **1 Samuel 8:20** - "Then we will be like all of the other nations"
   - Identity/state: `be like {noun-phrase}`

5. **1 Samuel 10:24** - "No man is like Saul"
   - Uniqueness statement: `is like {name}`

**Analysis**: Pattern choice depends on complexity:
- `[just-like X]` - for full clause/action comparisons
- `[like X]` - for simple noun/adjective comparisons

## Rule 27: Comparative Marking - `[more-than X]`

### BEST 5 EXAMPLES

1. **2 Samuel 13:15** - "Amnon hated Tamar [more-than Amnon previously loved Tamar]"
   - Self-comparison across time
   - Pattern: `VERB [more-than SUBJECT PAST-VERB]`

2. **Genesis 25:28** - "Isaac loved Esau [more-than Isaac loved Jacob]"
   - Sibling comparison with same verb
   - Pattern: `VERB OBJ1 [more-than SUBJ VERB OBJ2]`

3. **Matthew 10:37** - "A person [who loves that person's father or mother [more-than that person loves me(Jesus)]]"
   - Relative clause with comparison
   - Theological priority statement

4. **Mark 13:19** - "People never suffered [more-than people will suffer in those days]"
   - Temporal/historical superlative
   - Pattern: `never VERB [more-than ...]`

5. **Matthew 11:22** - "God will punish you [more-than God will punish Tyre]"
   - Comparative judgment
   - Pattern: Future action comparison

### CONTRADICTIONS (Comparatives NOT marked with more-than)

**Alternative Pattern: "better than" / "greater than"**

1. **1 Samuel 15:28** - "That person is better than you(Saul)"
   - Uses: `better than`
   - Not using: `[more-than]` bracket syntax

2. **1 Samuel 24:17** - "You(David) are better than me(Saul)"
   - Simple adjective comparison

3. **2 Samuel 17:14** - "Hushai's plan is better than Ahithophel's plan"
   - Comparative adjective without bracketing

4. **Daniel 11:36** - "That king will think [that that king is greater than all gods]"
   - Uses: `greater than`
   - Embedded in thought clause

5. **Genesis 48:19** - "Manasseh's younger brother will be greater than Manasseh"
   - Simple future prediction

**Analysis**: Two systems:
- `[more-than X]` - for verbal/action comparisons ("loved more-than")
- `better/greater than` - for adjectival/state comparisons ("is better than")

## Rule 28: Title/Section Markers - `(title)`

### BEST 5 EXAMPLES

1. **1 Samuel 2:12** - "(title) The sons of Eli are bad."
   - Introduces new character focus section

2. **1 Samuel 17:1** - "(title) David fights Goliath."
   - Major narrative episode marker

3. **1 Samuel 3:1** - "(title) God calls Samuel."
   - Pivotal divine interaction scene

4. **1 Samuel 25:36** - "(title) Nabal dies."
   - Event-focused section heading

5. **1 Samuel 12:1** - "(title) Samuel goodbyes the Israelites."
   - Major speech/farewell section

### PATTERN OBSERVATIONS

**When used:**
- At the start of major narrative episodes
- Before significant character introductions
- To mark topic/focus shifts
- For major events or speeches

**Format:**
- Always sentence-style with period
- Usually 3-6 words
- Present tense for ongoing action
- Can be character-focused, event-focused, or action-focused

## Rule 29: Paragraph Markers - `(paragraph)`, `_paragraph`

### BEST 5 EXAMPLES (Mid-verse usage)

1. **Mark 4:10** - "(paragraph) [(implicit-situational) After the crowd of people left,]"
   - Scene transition within verse
   - New time/location context

2. **Mark 7:6** - "(paragraph) Jesus said to those Pharisees and those people/scribes"
   - New speaker/dialogue section
   - Shifts from narrative to direct speech

3. **Mark 8:4** - "(paragraph) (rhetorical) Jesus's followers/disciples asked Jesus"
   - Question/answer exchange marker
   - Dialogue shift within conversation

4. **Mark 14:22** - "[While Jesus _paragraph and those followers/disciples were eating]"
   - Inline paragraph marker (underscore variant)
   - Temporal clause with topic shift

5. **Mark 13:5** - "(paragraph) Then Jesus said to Peter, James, John, and Andrew"
   - Major teaching section begins
   - New addressees/audience

### PATTERN OBSERVATIONS

**Two forms:**
- `(paragraph)` - Parenthetical, at start of sentence/clause
- `_paragraph` - Inline, embedded in ongoing clause

**Usage context:**
- Scene transitions
- Speaker changes
- Major topic shifts
- Time/location changes within verse

## Rule 30: Footnote System - `(footnote)`

### BEST 5 CROSS-REFERENCE EXAMPLES

1. **Mark 13:25** - "(footnote) You(people) (imp) see Isaiah 13:10, Isaiah 34:4, and Joel 2:10"
   - Multiple OT references for prophetic imagery
   - Pattern: `see {ref}, {ref}, and {ref}`

2. **Mark 14:62** - "(footnote) You(people) (imp) see Psalms 110:1 and Daniel 7:13"
   - Messianic prophecy citations
   - Links Jesus's statement to OT sources

3. **Mark 14:27** - "(footnote) You(people) (imp) see Zechariah 13:7"
   - Single clear quotation source

4. **Mark 10:23** - "(footnote) You(people) (imp) see Deuteronomy 24:1"
   - Legal/Torah reference

5. **Mark 13:26** - "(footnote) You(followers) (imp) see Daniel 7:13"
   - Apocalyptic vision reference

### BEST 5 EXPLANATORY NOTE EXAMPLES

1. **1 Samuel 1:20** - "Samuel (footnote) sounds like ['God heard me(Hannah)'] in Hebrew"
   - Etymology/wordplay explanation
   - Pattern: `{name} (footnote) sounds like ["{meaning}"] in {language}`

2. **1 Samuel 4:21** - "Ichabod (footnote) means no glory"
   - Name meaning
   - Pattern: `{name} (footnote) means {meaning}`

3. **Acts 17:7** - "Caesar (footnote) is the title for the king of Rome. At that time, the King of Rome was named Claudius"
   - Cultural/historical context
   - Two-part: title explanation + specific historical detail

4. **Mark 9:5** - "(footnote) Rabbi named the word _implicitType of Hebrew means-C teacher"
   - Foreign word translation
   - Language identification + meaning

5. **Genesis 5:1** - "(footnote) the people were like God"
   - Theological/interpretive note
   - Clarifies "image of God" concept

### PATTERN OBSERVATIONS

**Two main types:**
1. **Cross-references**: `You(people) (imp) see {Scripture-ref}`
2. **Explanatory**:
   - Name meanings: `{name} (footnote) means {meaning}`
   - Etymology: `sounds like ["{phrase}"] in {language}`
   - Cultural context: Historical/linguistic background
   - Interpretive notes: Clarifies theological concepts

## Rule 31: Alternative Readings - `(alt)`, `(primary)`, `(meaning-1)`

### BEST 5 VARIANT EXAMPLES

1. **Mark 1:2** - "(primary) I(God) will send my messenger... (meaning-1) I(God) will send my messenger in-front-of you"
   - Translation variant for same Hebrew text
   - Primary vs. alternative interpretation of "before you"

2. **Mark 14:22** - "(primary) This bread is my(Jesus's) body. (meaning-1) This bread is like my(Jesus's) body"
   - Literal vs. figurative interpretation
   - Theological significance: real presence vs. symbolic

3. **Matthew 8:7** - "(primary) Jesus said... 'I will come...' (meaning-1) Jesus said... 'Do you want me to come...?'"
   - Statement vs. question reading
   - Greek syntax allows both interpretations

4. **Matthew 19:11** - "(primary) ...people caused by God to be able to not marry... (meaning-1) ...people caused by God to be able to accept these words"
   - Referent ambiguity: celibacy vs. acceptance of teaching
   - Pronoun/antecedent uncertainty

5. **1 Samuel 20:16** - Main text: "I hope Yahweh will punish David's enemies" → (alt) "I hope Yahweh will punish David's family [if David does not treat me kindly]"
   - Conditional vs. unconditional reading
   - Covenant blessing/curse formula variation

### PATTERN OBSERVATIONS

**Usage contexts:**
- Ambiguous syntax (statement vs. question)
- Multiple valid translations of same source
- Literal vs. figurative interpretations
- Theological implications require both readings
- Textual variants in manuscripts

**Marker hierarchy:**
- `(primary)` - Most likely/preferred interpretation
- `(meaning-1)` - First alternative meaning
- `(alt)` - Alternative reading (less common marker)

**Integration:**
- Can appear inline with main text
- Sometimes presented as separate sentences
- Often highlights interpretive tradition differences

## SUMMARY: Rule Implementation Consistency

| Rule | Primary Pattern | Alternative Pattern | Deciding Factor |
|------|----------------|---------------------|-----------------|
| **6: Named intro** | `X named Y` | `The name of X was Y` | Context: inline vs. formal/genealogical |
| **26: Similes** | `[just-like X]` | `[like X]` | Complexity: full clause vs. simple noun comparison |
| **27: Comparatives** | `[more-than X]` | `better/greater than` | Type: verbal/action vs. adjectival/state |
| **28: Titles** | `(title) {sentence}` | N/A | Consistent usage |
| **29: Paragraphs** | `(paragraph)` | `_paragraph` | Position: start vs. inline |
| **30: Footnotes** | `(footnote) see {ref}` | `(footnote) means {X}` | Type: cross-ref vs. explanatory |
| **31: Alternatives** | `(primary)` + `(meaning-1)` | `(alt)` | Hierarchy of interpretations |

**Key Finding**: Most "contradictions" are actually contextual variations - TBTA uses different markers based on grammatical context, semantic complexity, and discourse function rather than arbitrary choice.
