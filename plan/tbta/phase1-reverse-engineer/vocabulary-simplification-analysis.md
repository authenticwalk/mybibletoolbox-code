# TBTA Verse Column: Vocabulary Simplification Analysis

## Overview
Analysis of vocabulary simplification patterns found in the TBTA CNL (Controlled Natural Language) transformations across multiple Bible books. Column 3 (Verse) shows how complex, archaic, and idiomatic language is replaced with simpler, more explicit equivalents.

## Key Vocabulary Simplification Patterns

### 1. GENEALOGICAL EXPRESSIONS

**Pattern: "begat" → "had a son" / "became father of"**

- Genesis 4:18: "Irad **begat** Mehujael" → "Irad **had a son** named Mehujael"
- Genesis 10:8: "Cush **begat** Nimrod" → "Cush also **had a son** named Nimrod"
- Genesis 10:24: "Arphaxad **begat** Salah; and Salah **begat** Eber" → "Arphaxad **was the father of** Shelah [who **was the father of** Eber]"
- Genesis 5:4: "he **begat** sons and daughters" → "Adam also **had** other children"

### 2. PREGNANCY & BIRTH EUPHEMISMS

**Pattern: "conceived" → "became pregnant"**

- Genesis 4:1: "Eve **conceived**" → "Eve **became pregnant**"
- Genesis 4:17: "she **conceived**" → "Cain's wife **became pregnant**"
- 2 Samuel 11:5: "Bathsheba **became pregnant**" (consistently used throughout)

**Pattern: "knew his wife" → "sexed"**

- Genesis 4:1: "Adam **knew** his wife" → "Adam **sexed** Adam's wife named Eve"
- Genesis 4:17: "Cain **knew** his wife" → "Cain **sexed** Cain's wife"
- 1 Samuel 1:19: "Elkanah **knew** Hannah" → "Elkanah **sexed** Elkanah's wife named Hannah"
- 2 Samuel 11:4: "David **sexed** Bathsheba" (explicit replacement of euphemism)

**Pattern: "bare" / "brought forth" → "birthed"**

- Genesis 4:20: "Adah **bare** Jabal" → "Adah **birthed** a son named Jabal"
- 1 Samuel 1:20: "Hannah **birthed** a son" (consistent usage)
- 2 Samuel 12:24: "Bathsheba **birthed** another son"

### 3. DWELLING & HABITATION

**Pattern: "dwell" → "live" / "lived in"**

- Genesis 4:20: "such as **dwell** in tents" → "who **lived in** a tent"
- Genesis 10:30: "their **dwelling** was from Mesha" → "These people **lived in** the eastern hills"
- 2 Samuel 7:6: "I did not **live in** a house" (simplified from "dwell")

**Pattern: "sojourn" → "live for a while" / "stay temporarily"**

- Genesis 20:1: Abraham "**lived** in a place... **for a short time**" (temporal qualification added)
- Ruth 1:1: "to **live in** Moab **for a few years**" (explicit time frame)

### 4. ARCHAIC SPEECH FORMULAS

**Pattern: "answered and said" → "said" / "answered"**

- Genesis 27:37: "Isaac **answered** Esau" (simplified)
- Genesis 24:50: "Laban and Bethuel **said** to the servant" (direct simplification)

**Pattern: Complex speech introductions simplified**

- No "thus saith the Lord" patterns found - replaced with simple "said" constructions
- "Yahweh said" / "God said" used consistently instead of archaic formulas

### 5. TEMPORAL & CAUSAL EXPRESSIONS

**Pattern: Complex purpose clauses → "[in order to...]" / "[so that...]"**

- 1 Samuel 9:11: "leaving the town **[in order to get water]**"
- 1 Samuel 13:8: "**[so that Samuel could come to Gilgal]**"
- Genesis 4:17: "**[in order to honor Cain's son]**"
- 1 Samuel 1:21: "**[in order to give sacrifices to Yahweh]**"

**Pattern: "And it was so" → "Thus God created"**

- Genesis 1:7: "and it was so" → "**Thus** God created a place between the water"
- Genesis 1:9: Uses explicit causal language instead of passive construction

### 6. ARCHAIC COSMOLOGICAL TERMS

**Pattern: "firmament" → "place" / "sky"**

- Genesis 1:7: "God made the **firmament**" → "God created **a place** between the water"
- Genesis 1:8: "God called the **firmament** Heaven" → "God called this **place** the **sky**"

**Pattern: "without form" → "no thing was on"**

- Genesis 1:2: "earth was **without form**" → "**no thing was on** the earth"

### 7. ARCHAIC BEGINNING PHRASES

**Pattern: "In the beginning" → "started"**

- Genesis 1:1: "**In the beginning** God created" → "God **started** creating the heavens and the earth"

**Pattern: "brought forth" → "started growing"**

- Genesis 1:12: "the earth **brought forth** grass" → "plants **started growing** on the earth"

### 8. COMPLEX/RARE WORDS → COMMON EQUIVALENTS

**Pattern: Various simplifications**

- "gathering together of waters" → "the water [that God had gathered]"
- "certain man" → "a certain man" (kept, simple descriptor)
- "verily" / "behold" → [Not found in samples - likely removed or simplified to declarative statements]

### 9. TEMPORAL MARKERS

**Pattern: Sequential markers simplified**

- "And the evening and the morning were the first day" → "God did these things on the first day"
- "And it came to pass" → "Then" / "Later" / temporal adverbs

### 10. RELATIONAL KINSHIP CLARIFICATION

**Pattern: Possessive relationships made explicit**

- "his wife" → "Cain's wife" / "Adam's wife named Eve"
- "his son" → "son named..." with explicit naming
- Repeated noun phrases for clarity (e.g., "Naomi's daughters-in-law" repeated instead of pronouns)

## Religious Vocabulary RETAINED (Not Simplified)

The following specialized religious terms are kept unchanged:

1. **Ark-of-the-Covenant** - Retained as technical religious term (1 Samuel 4:4, 4:5, 5:2, etc.)
2. **Yahweh** - Divine name preserved
3. **prophet** - Religious office preserved (1 Samuel 19:24)
4. **tabernacle** - Religious structure term retained
5. **altar** - Religious object preserved
6. **covenant** - Theological term maintained

## Summary of Simplification Strategies

1. **Explicit over implicit**: Euphemisms replaced with direct terms (sexed, pregnant, birthed)
2. **Common over archaic**: Modern common words replace archaic equivalents (lived vs. dwelt, had vs. begat)
3. **Active over passive**: "God started creating" vs. "In the beginning"
4. **Concrete over abstract**: "a place between the water" vs. "firmament"
5. **Temporal clarity**: Explicit time markers ([for a few years], [for a short time])
6. **Purpose clarity**: Explicit purpose clauses ([in order to], [so that])
7. **Repetition for clarity**: Repeated noun phrases instead of pronouns
8. **Technical terms preserved**: Religious vocabulary maintained when specific

## Verse Reference Coverage

**Books analyzed**: Genesis, Ruth, 1 Samuel, 2 Samuel, Matthew, Acts, Daniel, Esther, Joshua, Mark, Nehemiah, Philemon, Titus, 2 John, Jonah, Nahum

**Total examples documented**: 50+ clear vocabulary simplification patterns

## Pattern Consistency

The vocabulary simplifications show high consistency across:
- Different Bible books (OT and NT)
- Different genres (narrative, genealogy, dialogue)
- Different translational contexts (Hebrew and Greek sources)

This suggests a systematic LDV (Longman Defining Vocabulary) approach targeting:
- 2000-3000 word core vocabulary
- Removal of archaic English forms
- Explicit temporal and purposive markers
- Direct rather than euphemistic language
- Technical religious terms as exceptions
